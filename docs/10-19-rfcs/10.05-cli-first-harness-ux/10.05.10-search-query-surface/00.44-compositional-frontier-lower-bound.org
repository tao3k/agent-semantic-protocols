#+title: RFC 10.05.10.00.44 — Compositional Frontier Lower Bound
#+startup: overview

* Status

Draft research specification.

This RFC makes the residual closure lower bound from RFC 00.43 compositional
over AND/OR proof frontiers. It does not add CLI commands or authorize
implementation.

* Problem

RFC 00.43 rejects an inspect split when:

#+begin_quote
=query cost + residual closure lower bound > remaining budget=.
#+end_quote

That rule is sound only when the residual lower bound is itself proven.
AND/OR frontiers do not share one composition rule.

For an AND frontier, every required child must be realized. It is tempting to
add child lower bounds. That is unsound when the children share an evidence
witness: each child can have cost 10 while the combined proof DAG costs 10, not
20.

For an OR frontier, one branch is selected. Adding branch bounds is plainly
wrong; a safe lower bound is the minimum over eligible alternatives.

* Decision

Residual lower bounds are objective-specific proof objects with explicit
frontier composition:

- AND with unknown sharing: use the maximum child lower bound;
- AND with proven identity decomposition: add the shared component once and
  add each exclusive component;
- AND with proven disjointness: child lower bounds may be added;
- OR alternatives: use the minimum eligible child lower bound;
- infeasible OR branches may be removed only with feasibility certificates.

No implementation may infer disjointness from different graph paths, node
addresses, or provider-local object identity.

* Lower-bound contract

For a scalar objective, =LowerBound(lower, actual)= means:

#+begin_quote
=lower <= actual=.
#+end_quote

The certificate binds:

- objective identity and version;
- evidence-root and source-index generation;
- semantic and model-cache context where relevant;
- frontier obligation identity;
- sharing decomposition identity; and
- feasibility context.

The full canonical lexicographic vector requires its own versioned bound
relation. Scalar arithmetic MUST NOT be silently applied to the vector.

* AND frontier with unknown sharing

If combined proof cost is at least each required child cost, and each child has
a valid lower bound, then:

#+begin_quote
=max(left lower, right lower) <= combined cost=.
#+end_quote

This bound remains sound under arbitrary lawful sharing. It may be weak.

Weakness is acceptable for candidate ordering or incomplete completion. It may
prevent exact frontier closure, in which case SearchLoop can inspect sharing
identity or retain an open frontier.

* AND frontier with disjointness

If identity evidence proves the child witness sets are disjoint and combined
cost is their additive composition, then:

#+begin_quote
=left lower + right lower <= combined cost=.
#+end_quote

Different labels or traversal branches do not prove disjointness. Complete
evidence identities from RFC 00.38 determine overlap.

* Identity-aware AND decomposition

For overlapping children, partition required evidence into:

- shared identities;
- left-exclusive identities;
- right-exclusive identities.

The lower bound is:

#+begin_quote
=shared lower + left-exclusive lower + right-exclusive lower=.
#+end_quote

Shared cost is charged once. The decomposition certificate proves coverage,
pairwise exclusivity of the three partitions, and identity equality for every
shared occurrence.

This gives a stronger safe bound than =max= without double-counting.

* Shared-witness counterexample

Both child obligations use exactly the same 10-token witness.

- left child lower bound: 10;
- right child lower bound: 10;
- combined proof-DAG cost: 10;
- naïve additive lower bound: 20.

Because =20 <= 10= is false, adding child bounds without disjointness is not a
lower bound. The safe unknown-sharing bound is =max(10, 10) = 10=.

* OR frontier

For two eligible alternatives with lower bounds =left= and =right=:

#+begin_quote
=min(left, right)=
#+end_quote

is a lower bound for whichever branch is selected.

An infeasible branch can make the minimum weak. It may be excluded only when a
feasibility certificate is bound to the same budget, ledger, capability, and
cache context.

An unavailable provider is not silently treated as infinite cost unless its
unavailability is certified for the active generation.

* Empty frontiers

- an empty AND frontier is already realized and has lower bound zero;
- an empty OR frontier is unrealizable, not zero-cost.

The two cases MUST remain distinct in schema and Lean types. Returning zero for
an empty OR frontier would turn absence of a route into a free route.

* SearchLoop consequence

A residual-bound receipt exposes:

#+begin_src json
{
  "frontierKind": "and",
  "boundMode": "identity-aware",
  "sharedLowerBound": 10,
  "exclusiveLowerBounds": [0, 0],
  "residualLowerBound": 10,
  "sharingCertificateDigest": "..."
}
#+end_src

When only the safe =max= bound is available, the packet says
=boundMode: unknown-sharing=. The agent can decide whether a sharing inspection
is worth another round.

* Python Turbo Graph consequence

The future Python graph layer SHOULD compute:

- frontier type;
- child lower bounds;
- identity-set incidence;
- shared/exclusive partitions;
- feasibility-filtered OR alternatives; and
- bound-strength metadata.

Sparse set/intersection operations can accelerate identity decomposition. They
do not replace the identity and coverage proofs.

* Required Lean obligations

The companion Lean model MUST prove:

- maximum child lower bound is sound for required children under unknown
  sharing;
- additive child bounds are sound under additive disjoint composition;
- the shared-witness example refutes naïve addition;
- identity-aware decomposition charges the shared component once;
- minimum child lower bound is sound for either OR selection; and
- the unknown-sharing and identity-aware example bounds equal the actual
  combined cost.

The modular audit emits these theorem families as Lean-generated JSON.

* Design finding

RFC 00.43 treated =residualClosureLowerBound= as one certified number. Lean
forces the certificate to explain how child bounds compose. The shared-witness
counterexample shows that AND does not imply addition; proof-DAG identity
sharing is part of lower-bound soundness.
