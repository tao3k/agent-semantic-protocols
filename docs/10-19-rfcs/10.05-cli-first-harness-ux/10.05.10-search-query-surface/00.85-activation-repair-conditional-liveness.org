#+title: RFC 10.05.85 — Activation Repair Conditional Liveness
#+subtitle: Explicit progress assumptions, durable publication, and bounded repair completion
#+language: en
#+options: toc:3 num:t

* Status

Draft for Lean-backed architectural audit.

RFC 10.05.84 proves repair admission and one-winner safety. This RFC defines
the environmental assumptions under which an admitted repair can make bounded
progress. It does not promote those assumptions into unconditional facts.

* Live counterexample

The development environment currently demonstrates the distinction between
activation state and executable progress:

- provider inspection reports the Org provider as =activated=;
- the authoritative resident registry for the root session contains no
  resident sessions;
- an exact Org owner search prescribes resuming =asp-explore=;
- that resident does not exist in the registry; and
- policy correctly forbids raw source fallback.

Thus =activated= does not imply =CanAdvance=. A lifecycle protocol needs a
proof of the next authorized transition, not merely a label describing a past
activation.

The same inspection also reports no Lean language facade. A proof package may
exist and compile while the search control plane has no parser-owned Lean
discovery route. Provider availability is therefore a capability relation, not
a repository-wide Boolean.

* Progress milestones

Repair completion is decomposed into observable stages:

#+begin_src typst
$ "needs-repair"
  arrow.r
  "capability-issued"
  arrow.r
  "candidate-committed"
  arrow.r
  "publication-enqueued"
  arrow.r
  "candidate-published"
  arrow.r
  "host-observed"
  arrow.r
  "dispatch-reconciled"
  arrow.r
  "trusted-ready"
  arrow.r
  "rollback-closed"
  arrow.r
  "retired" $

"ok"
#+end_src

No stage may be collapsed into another:

- candidate commit is not durable publication;
- durable enqueue is not delivery;
- delivery is not host observation;
- host observation is not dispatch reconciliation;
- reconciliation is not fresh readiness;
- readiness is not rollback-window closure; and
- rollback closure is not durable retirement.

* Conditional liveness proposition

Let $Step_Gamma$ be the transition relation whose edge constructors require
the corresponding member of $Gamma$.

#+name: activation-repair-conditional-liveness-proposition
#+begin_src typst
$ (
  A and C and S and O and P and H and D and F and R and G
)
arrow.r.double
"Reachable"_Gamma("needs-repair", "retired") $

$ "Step"_Gamma(x, y)
arrow.r.double
mu(y) < mu(x) $

$ mu("needs-repair") = 9
quad
mu("retired") = 0 $

"ok"
#+end_src

The first proposition is conditional reachability. The second gives a strictly
decreasing finite ranking function, excluding cycles from the successful
milestone path. Neither proposition proves that the environment satisfies
$Gamma$.

* Normative requirements

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ARCL-NEXT

An activation status MUST NOT be treated as progress evidence unless the
current state has an authorized executable next transition.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ARCL-AUTHORITY

Repair liveness MUST state how authority becomes reachable independently of
the failed activation closure. Missing authority is a terminal progress
blocker, not a retryable resident-search result.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ARCL-CAPABILITY

A capability used for repair MUST remain valid through the linearization point.
Expiry or revocation before commit invalidates progress and requires a new
proposal from a fresh head.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ARCL-SCHEDULER

One admissible repair writer MUST eventually be scheduled. CAS guarantees a
single winner but does not guarantee that any writer runs.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ARCL-OUTBOX

Candidate commit and publication enqueue MUST be distinct durable milestones.
The commit transaction MUST leave replayable intent when immediate enqueue is
unavailable.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ARCL-DELIVERY

Outbox durability does not imply delivery. Delivery liveness MUST name a
fairness or bounded-retry assumption and MUST preserve idempotent sink
acceptance.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ARCL-HOST-LEASE

Host reachability MUST be represented by an expiring observation lease bound to
candidate generation and authority revision. A historical host observation
cannot advance the current repair.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ARCL-DISPATCH

Quarantine admits safe repair but does not discharge the old dispatch.
=TrustedReady= MUST require terminal reconciliation or a separately proven
effect-isolation policy.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ARCL-FRESHNESS

Readiness evidence MUST be reacquired after publication and MUST bind the
current authority revision. Repair commit invalidates every earlier readiness
observation.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ARCL-ROLLBACK

The rollback window MUST use an explicit monotone clock or revision source.
Silence and elapsed wall-clock assumptions without authority evidence MUST NOT
retire the previous generation.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ARCL-RETIREMENT

Rollback closure and retirement persistence are separate operations. Failed
generation garbage collection MUST occur only after durable retirement.

* Lean model

The executable model is:

- =packages/proofs/ASPProof/ActivationRepairLiveness.lean=

It defines:

- ten observable repair stages;
- ten explicit environmental guarantees;
- a constructor-owned transition relation;
- a transitive reachability relation;
- a decreasing nine-step ranking function;
- conditional reachability from =needsRepair= to =retired=; and
- counterexamples for missing authority, unstable capability, unavailable
  scheduler, unavailable outbox, unfair publication, unavailable host lease,
  unreconciled dispatch, missing fresh evidence, a frozen rollback clock, and
  unavailable retirement persistence.

* Logic bugs found

** Activated is not executable

Activation status records configuration history. It does not prove that the
required resident, authority, transport, or parser-owned route currently
exists. The lifecycle API needs =CanAdvance(state)= evidence.

** CAS safety is orthogonal to scheduling liveness

One-winner linearization prevents duplicate winners. It cannot make an
admissible writer execute. Scheduler fairness must be visible.

** Durable intent and delivery were previously conflated

A committed candidate may be lost from the publication path if outbox enqueue
is not independently durable. Conversely, a durable outbox can remain
undelivered forever. The two milestones require separate evidence.

** Quarantine postpones an obligation

Quarantine is not reconciliation. Permitting =TrustedReady= solely because old
work was quarantined can hide duplicate external effects indefinitely.

** Rollback time was underspecified

A “rollback window” has no protocol meaning without a monotone authority-bound
clock or revision. Local wall time cannot safely authorize retirement under
clock drift or suspended execution.

* Remaining proof boundary

The finite model proves bounded milestone reachability under a complete
guarantee vector. It does not yet model:

- guarantee leases expiring between transitions;
- capability renewal concurrent with revocation;
- crash recovery between CAS commit and outbox enqueue;
- duplicate outbox delivery and sink acknowledgement;
- effect-isolation semantics for quarantined dispatch;
- multiple generations repairing concurrently; or
- garbage-collection safety after durable retirement.

The next proof should refine the commit/outbox boundary into an atomic durable
repair journal with replay, idempotent delivery, and crash-state
counterexamples.
