#+title: RFC 10.05.18 — Constructive SearchRoute Enumeration
#+startup: overview
#+options: toc:3

* Status

Draft.

This RFC advances the ranked-DAG model from an existence theorem to an
executable, fuel-bounded candidate generator.  Lean verifies the graph-walk
enumerator, derives a finite step-instance catalog from the route budget,
constructs the bounded route catalog, and proves bounded candidate
completeness.  It also exposes a missing finiteness condition in the route
model: token and round budgets alone do not bound the number of zero-cost
transitions.  The corrected graph-route budget therefore includes an explicit
transition ceiling.

This RFC is an amendment to:

- [[file:00.16-searchroute-bounded-shortest-path.org][RFC 10.05.16 — SearchRoute as a Bounded Shortest-Path Problem]]
- [[file:00.17-searchroute-ranked-dag-global-optimality.org][RFC 10.05.17 — Ranked-DAG Global Optimality]]

It does not authorize a Rust implementation.  The current scope is the RFC,
Lean model, executable proof audit, AXLE receipt, and Org-Babel Typst
exposition.

* Problem

RFC 10.05.17 proves that a locally selected candidate is globally no worse
when the generated candidate list is complete and the selected candidate is a
realization of the requested source-target pair.  That theorem is logically
sound, but its =CandidateComplete= premise was abstract.

Making that premise constructive reveals two independent enumeration problems:

1. enumerate every bounded graph walk from the current source;
2. enumerate every admissible SearchLoop route that may realize an endpoint.

A finite ranked graph solves only the first problem.  It does not imply that
the route space is finite.

* Design decision

** Graph enumeration is fuel-bounded and executable
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-SRE-WALK-ENUMERATOR
:END:

For a ranked DAG =G=, source =s=, and fuel =f=,
=enumerateWalkEndpoints G s f= emits the stop case =(s, 0)= and recursively
extends every outgoing edge while decreasing fuel.

The result is a finite list of endpoint and graph-hop-count pairs.  It is
suitable for an agent-facing SearchLoop because the generator exposes only the
current bounded frontier; it does not require a human-oriented CLI command
language for predeclaring a complete search plan.

** The enumerator is sound
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-SRE-WALK-SOUNDNESS
:END:

Every emitted pair =(t, h)= has a corresponding =Walk G s t h=.  The
enumerator cannot manufacture an endpoint, an edge, or a hop receipt that is
absent from the ranked graph.

This property is the formal boundary between a router hint and a validated
graph path.  Only sound enumerator output may set =pathValidated = true=.

** The enumerator is complete for bounded walks
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-SRE-WALK-COMPLETENESS
:END:

Every walk from =s= to =t= using =h <= f= graph hops appears as =(t, h)= in
the enumerator output.

Because every edge strictly increases rank, any walk starting at =s= has at
most =maxRank - rank(s)= hops.  Therefore the canonical fuel

#+begin_example
maxRank - rank(source)
#+end_example

is sufficient to enumerate every graph walk reachable from =source=.

** Candidate generation has two stages
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-SRE-TWO-STAGE-COMPLETENESS
:END:

The constructive candidate set is the product of:

- enumerated endpoint-hop receipts; and
- a finite route catalog.

The product is globally candidate-complete only when both stages are complete.
Graph-walk completeness cannot substitute for route-catalog completeness.

** A finite step catalog constructs the route catalog
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-SRE-ROUTE-CATALOG
:END:

A finite =stepCatalog= and =maxTransitions= construct every bounded step list.
The recursive enumerator emits the empty list at every fuel level and prepends
each catalog step to every list emitted with one less unit of fuel.  Lean proves
that every step list of length at most =maxTransitions= whose elements occur in
=stepCatalog= is emitted.

For each step list, the route generator constructs the four combinations of
=graphEscalated= and =semanticCacheValidated=.  The other metadata fields are
fixed by feasibility:

- =domain = requirement.activeDomain=;
- =closureDigest = requirement.expectedClosure=;
- =closed = true=.

Filtering these variants with =feasibleB= produces
=enumerateFeasibleRoutes=.  Lean proves that this list satisfies
=RouteCatalogComplete=.

The transition premise is part of the completeness interface, not merely a
later candidate filter.  An earlier draft added =maxTransitions= to
=graphFeasibleB= but left it out of =RouteCatalogComplete=; Lean review exposed
that mismatch.  Without the premise, the catalog would still have to contain
every arbitrarily padded route admitted by the old =RouteBudget=.

The graph and route enumerators combine into a constructive
=CandidateComplete= theorem.

*** Budget-derived step-instance coverage
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-SRE-STEP-INSTANCE-CATALOG
:END:

The route budget already bounds every field of a step appearing in a feasible
route:

- =evidenceTokens <= maxEvidenceTokens=;
- =promptTokens <= maxRawTokens=;
- =cachedPromptTokens <= promptTokens <= maxRawTokens=;
- =roundCost <= maxRounds=.

The first, second, and fourth bounds follow because a member's non-negative
cost cannot exceed the corresponding route total.  The cached-prompt bound
also uses =prefixCacheSoundB=.  =StepKind= is a finite eight-constructor type.

=enumerateRouteSteps= therefore constructs the finite Cartesian product of all
step kinds and all numeric fields within those bounds.  Lean proves:

#+begin_example
StepCatalogComplete
  requirement
  routeBudget
  (enumerateRouteSteps routeBudget)
#+end_example

Substituting this theorem into the route and graph enumerators removes the last
external catalog-completeness premise from bounded =CandidateComplete=.

** Completeness does not require eager materialization

The Lean list enumerators define finite extensional semantics.  They are a
specification oracle, not a requirement to dump the Cartesian product into an
agent context.

An online SearchLoop may implement the same candidate set as a lazy frontier:

- enumerate only the currently selected graph branch;
- expose a compact count, lower bound, and continuation receipt;
- materialize a route candidate only when its lower bound can still improve
  the current optimum;
- retain a coverage receipt for pruned or deferred branches; and
- stop when every remaining lower bound is no better than the selected route.

This preserves the constructive completeness theorem while minimizing search
commands, graph transitions, uncached model tokens, and agent/model rounds.
The interactive =inspect= operation should reveal the next bounded frontier,
not serialize every proof-level candidate.

** Transition count is a first-class budget
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-SRE-TRANSITION-BOUND
:END:

=GraphRouteBudget= includes =maxTransitions= in addition to =maxGraphHops= and
the existing token and round budgets.

The distinction is mandatory:

- =graphHops= counts validated semantic-graph edges;
- =transitionCount= counts SearchLoop route steps;
- =roundCost= counts agent/model interaction rounds.

These quantities may correlate in a particular implementation, but none is a
definition of either of the others.

The transition ceiling serves two purposes:

1. it makes finite route enumeration possible when the step alphabet and step
   fields are finite;
2. it prevents zero-token internal actions from creating an unbounded loop
   whose token receipt appears harmless.

* Lean-discovered counterexamples

** Zero-cost padding defeats the old route budget

Let =z= be an inspect step with zero evidence tokens, prompt tokens, cached
prompt tokens, and round cost.  Let =R(n)= be a valid router-jump route with =n=
copies of =z= inserted before closure.

Lean proves:

#+begin_example
forall n, feasibleB requirement generousRouteBudget (R n) = true
transitionCount (R n) = n + 2
#+end_example

Consequently, for every proposed transition ceiling =L=, the old route budget
admits a route with more than =L= transitions.  Token and round bounds do not
imply route-space finiteness.

** Graph completeness does not imply candidate completeness

A one-node ranked graph has a trivially complete graph-walk enumeration.  A
candidate generator that pairs its sole endpoint only with =routerJumpRoute=
still omits a feasible realization using =baselineLoopRoute=.

Lean proves both that the omitted candidate is feasible and that the resulting
candidate list is not =CandidateComplete=.

** The corrected budget rejects excess padding

For =R(maxTransitions + 1)=, =graphFeasibleB= evaluates to =false= even though
the route's token and round costs remain within the underlying =RouteBudget=.
The rejection is caused specifically by the transition ceiling.

= Constructive SearchRoute Enumeration

Let $G = (V, E, rho, rho_max)$ be a finite ranked DAG:

$ forall u, v in V: E(u, v) => rho(u) < rho(v), quad
  forall v in V: rho(v) <= rho_max. $

For a source $s$, define the canonical fuel

$ F(s) = rho_max - rho(s). $

== Fuel-bounded graph enumeration

Let $"Enum"(s, f)$ be a finite multiset of endpoint-hop pairs:

$ "Enum"(s, 0) = {(s, 0)}, $

$ "Enum"(s, f + 1)
    = {(s, 0)}
      union union_(E(s, v)) {(t, h + 1) | (t, h) in "Enum"(v, f)}. $

Soundness:

$ (t, h) in "Enum"(s, f) => "Walk"_G(s, t, h). $

Bounded completeness:

$ "Walk"_G(s, t, h) and h <= f => (t, h) in "Enum"(s, f). $

Rank progress gives

$ "Walk"_G(s, t, h) => rho(s) + h <= rho(t) <= rho_max, $

therefore

$ "Walk"_G(s, t, h) => h <= F(s). $

Thus $"Enum"(s, F(s))$ contains every walk reachable from $s$.

== Two-stage candidate construction

Let $B_R = (E_"max", T_"raw", T_"uncached", R_"max")$ be a route budget.
The finite budget-derived step-instance catalog is

$ C_S(B_R)
    = "StepKind"
      times [0, E_"max"]
      times [0, T_"raw"]
      times [0, T_"raw"]
      times [0, R_"max"]. $

For every feasible route $r$ and every step $x in "Steps"(r)$,

$ x."evidence" <= E_"max", $
$ x."prompt" <= T_"raw", $
$ x."cached" <= x."prompt" <= T_"raw", $
$ x."rounds" <= R_"max". $

Therefore

$ "StepCatalogComplete"(C_S(B_R), q, B_R). $

Let $N_"max"$ be the explicit transition ceiling.  Define the finite bounded
step-list enumerator:

$ "Seq"(C_S, 0) = {[]}, $

$ "Seq"(C_S, n + 1)
    = {[]} union
      {x :: "xs" | x in C_S and "xs" in "Seq"(C_S, n)}. $

Its completeness theorem is

$ "len"("xs") <= n
  and (forall x in "xs": x in C_S)
  => "xs" in "Seq"(C_S, n). $

For each emitted step list, enumerate the four Boolean metadata variants.  The
domain, closure digest, and closed flag are fixed by the requirement.  Filter
the result by route feasibility:

$ C_R =
  {r in "Variants"_q("Seq"(C_S, N_"max")) |
   "RouteFeasible"(B_G, r)}. $

Lean derives

$ "RouteCatalogComplete"(C_R, B_G). $

Now define the graph-candidate product:

$ "Gen"(G, s, F(s), C_R)
    = { (r, h, "true") |
        exists t: (t, h) in "Enum"(s, F(s)) and r in C_R }. $

Both enumeration stages are constructive.  Therefore

$ "Walk"_G(s, t, h)
  and "Realizes"(r, s, t)
  and "GraphFeasible"(B_G, r, h)
  => (r, h, "true") in "Gen"(G, s, F(s), C_R). $

== Why token and round budgets are insufficient

Let $z$ be a zero-cost inspect transition and let

$ R(n) = ["router"] ++ "repeat"(z, n) ++ ["close"]. $

For every $n in bb(N)$,

$ "Evidence"(R(n)) = 5, $
$ "Prompt"(R(n)) = 8, $
$ "Uncached"(R(n)) = 13, $
$ "Rounds"(R(n)) = 2, $
$ "Transitions"(R(n)) = n + 2. $

Hence

$ forall L in bb(N), exists n in bb(N):
  "Feasible"("RouteBudget", R(n))
  and "Transitions"(R(n)) > L. $

The route space is not bounded by token and round costs.  The corrected budget
must contain an independent transition ceiling:

$ B_G = (B_"route", H_"max", N_"max"), $

$ "GraphFeasible"(B_G, r, h)
    := "PathValidated"(r)
       and h <= H_"max"
       and "Transitions"(r) <= N_"max"
       and "Feasible"(B_"route", r). $

For $n = N_"max" + 1$,

$ "GraphFeasible"(B_G, R(n), 0) = "false". $

== Optimization consequence

Only after both enumeration stages are complete may a local finite optimizer
justify a global claim:

$ "Selected"(c, "Gen") and "CandidateComplete"("Gen")
  => forall c': "GraphFeasible"(c') => c <=_"lex" c'. $

The lexicographic objective remains

$ ("graph-hops", "uncached-tokens", "rounds", "transitions"), $

so validated semantic distance is optimized before model-context cost,
interaction rounds, or SearchLoop command count.
#+end_src

* Agent-facing SearchLoop consequence

This model supports a small interactive surface instead of a large
human-oriented search command grammar:

1. the router presents a bounded, parser-owned frontier;
2. the agent selects or refines one frontier clause;
3. the system emits a validated endpoint-hop receipt;
4. the route catalog supplies a bounded interaction realization;
5. the optimizer selects the lexicographically shortest feasible candidate;
6. the loop either closes with evidence or exposes the next bounded frontier.

The protocol minimizes four different costs without conflating them:

- semantic graph distance;
- SearchLoop transition count;
- uncached model-context tokens, including model prefix-cache effects;
- agent/model interaction rounds.

The semantic search cache and the model prefix cache remain separate:

- a semantic search cache may reduce retrieval evidence tokens or avoid a
  repeated graph query only when its identity and freshness are validated;
- a model prefix-cache hit reduces uncached prompt tokens but does not validate
  semantic evidence or shorten the semantic graph path.

* Remaining proof obligations

The following claims are deliberately not made yet:

1. normalization preserves every admissibility, cache-soundness, token-cost,
   and realization property needed by the optimizer;
2. a lazy branch-and-bound frontier is extensionally equivalent to the complete
   list enumerator and retains a machine-checkable coverage receipt;
3. an enumerated endpoint is cryptographically or structurally bound to the
   exact graph snapshot used by the candidate receipt;
4. the interactive router policy minimizes expected cost under uncertain
   branch probabilities;
5. semantic search-cache identity and model prefix-cache identity compose
   without allowing one cache receipt to impersonate the other.

These are the next Lean-to-RFC research boundary.  The next iteration should
prove that lazy lower-bound pruning preserves the complete enumerator's optimum
without materializing its potentially large Cartesian product.
