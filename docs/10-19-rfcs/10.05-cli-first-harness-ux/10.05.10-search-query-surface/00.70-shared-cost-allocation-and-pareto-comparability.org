#+title: 10.05.10.00.70 Shared Cost Allocation and Pareto Comparability
#+startup: overview

* Status

Draft proof refinement for shared provider-call cost, synthesis cost, and
route-level Pareto comparison.  This document changes no Rust or Python code.

* Problem

A provider call may realize several distinct routes.  Its call-level tokens and
fan-in synthesis tokens are shared costs.  Copying the complete batch cost onto
every route double charges the execution.  Dividing the cost and dropping an
integer remainder underreports it.

Even a perfectly conserved allocation is not canonical.  Two allocation
policies can preserve the same total batch cost while assigning different costs
to the same route.  A policy change can reverse a route-level Pareto result.

Therefore:

#+begin_quote
Equal batch totals do not imply equal route costs, and route costs produced by
different allocation contexts are not Pareto-comparable.
#+end_quote

* Allocation certificate

A shared-cost allocation certificate binds:

- graph generation;
- allocation-policy identity;
- search-cache context;
- model-prefix-cache context;
- a duplicate-free route identity list;
- allocated tokens for each route;
- explicitly unallocated shared tokens;
- provider-call tokens;
- synthesis tokens.

The conservation equation is:

#+begin_quote
sum(route allocations) + shared tokens
= call tokens + synthesis tokens
#+end_quote

The =shared tokens= field is not a loss bucket.  It is an explicit batch-level
cost that remains visible when a policy cannot or should not assign every token
to one route.

* Double-charge rejection

For a batch costing 25 tokens and realizing two routes, assigning 25 tokens to
both routes reports 50 tokens.  This may be useful as an upper bound for an
isolated route, but it cannot be reported as a conserved allocation.

Upper-bound projections MUST use a distinct field and MUST NOT participate in
sum-based batch accounting.

* Underreport rejection

Splitting 25 tokens as 12 and 12 leaves one token.  The certificate remains
valid only when that remainder is recorded as one shared token.  Omitting it
breaks conservation.

* Pareto comparison class

Route-level allocated costs are comparable only when their certificates bind
the same:

- graph generation;
- allocation policy;
- search-cache context;
- model-prefix-cache context.

Completion identity, evidence trace, and other route-equivalence requirements
from earlier RFCs still apply.  This RFC defines only the additional cost
comparability boundary.

Search cache and model-prefix cache remain independent.  Matching one cache
context does not compensate for a mismatch in the other.

* Policy-dependent dominance

Consider the same 25-token batch:

- policy A allocates =alpha= 10 and =beta= 15;
- policy B allocates =alpha= 15 and =beta= 10.

Against a 13-token alternative, =alpha= wins under policy A and loses under
policy B.  Both allocations conserve the same total.  A graph router that
ignores the policy identity can therefore reverse Pareto pruning without any
change in executed work.

* Normative clauses

- =ASP-RFC-10.05-SCA-CONSERVATION=: every allocation certificate preserves the
  complete provider-call and synthesis-token cost.
- =ASP-RFC-10.05-SCA-DISTINCT=: allocated route identities are duplicate-free.
- =ASP-RFC-10.05-SCA-CONTEXT=: cost comparability binds graph, policy, search
  cache, and model-prefix cache identities.
- =ASP-RFC-10.05-SCA-BALANCED=: the balanced witness conserves its complete
  25-token cost.
- =ASP-RFC-10.05-SCA-REMAINDER=: an indivisible remainder remains explicit as
  shared cost.
- =ASP-RFC-10.05-SCA-DOUBLE=: assigning the full batch cost to every route is
  not a conserved allocation.
- =ASP-RFC-10.05-SCA-UNDERREPORT=: dropping the shared remainder underreports
  total cost.
- =ASP-RFC-10.05-SCA-TOTAL-INSUFFICIENT=: equal batch totals do not determine
  per-route allocations.
- =ASP-RFC-10.05-SCA-POLICY=: different allocation policies reject route-level
  cost comparison.
- =ASP-RFC-10.05-SCA-CACHE=: a cache-context mismatch rejects comparison even
  when the allocation policy matches.
- =ASP-RFC-10.05-SCA-PARETO=: allocation policy can reverse a route-level Pareto
  result.
- =ASP-RFC-10.05-SCA-REFLEXIVE=: a valid certificate is cost-context comparable
  with itself.

* SearchLoop receipt

The compact batch receipt SHOULD expose:

- total call and synthesis tokens;
- route allocations;
- explicit shared tokens;
- allocation policy digest;
- both independent cache-context digests;
- conservation status;
- batch-level and route-level cost projections as distinct fields.

SearchLoop may use the batch total for global budget enforcement and the
policy-bound route allocations for Pareto comparison.  It MUST NOT substitute
one projection for the other.

* Consequences for Python Turbo Graph

The future graph should persist shared cost as a batch node connected to route
realization edges.  Per-route allocations are policy-owned derived edges.  This
prevents a mutable scalar on each route from duplicating or losing shared cost.

Changing the allocation policy creates a new projection identity rather than
rewriting historical execution receipts.
