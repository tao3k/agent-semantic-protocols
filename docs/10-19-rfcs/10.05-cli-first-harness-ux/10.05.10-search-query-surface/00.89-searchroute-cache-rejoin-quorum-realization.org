#+title: SearchRoute Cache Rejoin Quorum Realization
#+subtitle: Weighted signer realization and failure-domain coverage after canonical membership commitment
#+options: toc:3 num:t

* Status

This document is a proof-backed refinement of the SearchLoop rejoin path.
It follows the canonical membership commitment defined by RFC 10.05.88.

The RFC is intentionally limited to protocol semantics, Lean verification,
and executable Typst mathematics. It does not authorize a Rust or Python
implementation.

* Problem

RFC 10.05.88 closes an identity ambiguity: member order cannot change the
committed membership digest, while quorum weight and minimum failure-domain
requirements must change it.

That result is necessary but insufficient. A verifier can possess the correct
membership digest and still accept a certificate whose signers:

- carry less voting weight than the committed threshold;
- cover fewer failure domains than the committed policy requires;
- include an identity absent from the committed membership;
- repeat a signer identity; or
- refer to a policy whose threshold cannot be realized by all available
  members.

The last case is a logic defect in the previous boundary. Positivity and unique
member identities make a policy syntactically well formed, but do not make its
quorum requirements feasible.

* Design objective

The verifier must distinguish three facts:

1. the membership policy has a stable committed identity;
2. the policy is feasible against its own total resources; and
3. this concrete signer set realizes both committed quorum dimensions.

These facts must remain separate in the audit surface. A digest match is not a
compressed substitute for quorum realization.

* Normative clauses

** CRQR-FEASIBLE: policy feasibility

A membership policy is feasible only when it is well formed and:

- its quorum weight is at most the sum of all committed member weights; and
- its minimum failure-domain count is at most the number of distinct committed
  failure domains.

The rejoin verifier MUST reject an infeasible policy before certificate
realization is considered.

** CRQR-SIGNER: signer-set validity

A signer set is valid only when signer identities are unique and every signer
resolves to a member of the committed policy.

Repeated signer identities MUST NOT multiply voting weight. Unknown signer
identities MUST NOT contribute weight or domain coverage and MUST cause
rejection.

** CRQR-WEIGHT: realized voting weight

Realized voting weight is the sum of the committed voting weights of selected
members. The certificate realizes the weight dimension only when that sum is
at least the committed quorum weight.

** CRQR-DOMAIN: realized failure-domain coverage

Realized failure-domain coverage is represented by an explicit, duplicate-free
domain witness carried by the certificate. Every witnessed domain must resolve
to at least one selected committed member. The certificate realizes the domain
dimension only when the witness length is at least the committed minimum.

Multiple signers in one failure domain contribute their voting weights, but
that domain contributes only one unit of domain coverage.

** CRQR-ACCEPT: conjunctive acceptance

A certificate is accepted only when all of the following hold:

- the membership policy is feasible;
- the certificate membership digest equals the digest of that policy;
- the signer set is valid;
- realized voting weight reaches the committed quorum threshold; and
- realized distinct-domain coverage reaches the committed domain threshold.

No one conjunct implies another.

** CRQR-REJECT: fail-closed adversarial cases

Insufficient weight, insufficient domain coverage, an unknown signer, or a
duplicate signer is independently sufficient for rejection.

** CRQR-GAP: well-formedness is not feasibility

The one-member policy with member weight 1, quorum weight 2, and one required
failure domain is well formed under RFC 10.05.88 but infeasible. This
counterexample requires the new feasibility gate; it is not merely an
implementation recommendation.

* Mathematical model

Let =P= name the committed membership policy, =S= the certificate signer set,
=Q(P)= its weight threshold, and =F(P)= its minimum distinct-domain threshold.
In prose these names only identify the model; the executable mathematical
content is owned by the following Org-Babel Typst block.

#+name: searchroute-cache-rejoin-quorum-realization-model
#+begin_src typst
#let feasible(total-weight, available-domains, quorum-weight, minimum-domains) = (
  quorum-weight <= total-weight
    and minimum-domains <= available-domains
)
#let realizes(
  signer-valid,
  realized-weight,
  realized-domains,
  quorum-weight,
  minimum-domains,
) = (
  signer-valid
    and quorum-weight <= realized-weight
    and minimum-domains <= realized-domains
)
#let accepts(
  policy-feasible,
  digest-matches,
  signer-valid,
  realized-weight,
  realized-domains,
  quorum-weight,
  minimum-domains,
) = (
  policy-feasible
    and digest-matches
    and realizes(
      signer-valid,
      realized-weight,
      realized-domains,
      quorum-weight,
      minimum-domains,
    )
)
#assert(feasible(4, 2, 3, 2))
#assert(not feasible(1, 1, 2, 1))
#assert(accepts(true, true, true, 4, 2, 3, 2))
#assert(not accepts(true, true, true, 2, 2, 3, 2))
#assert(not accepts(true, true, true, 4, 1, 3, 2))
#assert(not accepts(true, true, false, 4, 2, 3, 2))
"ok"
#+end_src

The acceptance proposition is:

#+name: searchroute-cache-rejoin-quorum-realization-proposition
#+begin_src typst
#let quorum-realization = $
  upright("Accept")(P, C)
  arrow.r.double
  upright("Feasible")(P)
  and D_C = D(P)
  and upright("ValidSigners")(P, S_C)
  and Q(P) <= W(P, S_C)
  and F(P) <= C(P, S_C)
$
#assert(type(quorum-realization) == content)
"ok"
#+end_src

Both blocks are evaluated from stdin with =typst eval=. They are not compiled
to side-effect files.

* Lean model

The proof owner is:

#+begin_example
packages/proofs/ASPProof/SearchRouteAdmissionRetryCacheRejoinQuorumRealization.lean
#+end_example

The model defines:

- =selectedMembers= as committed members selected by signer identity;
- =realizedVotingWeight= as the selected members' weight sum;
- =FailureDomainWitnessValid= as a duplicate-free list whose domains are each
  backed by a selected committed member;
- =MembershipPolicyFeasible= as well-formedness plus total-resource bounds;
- =SignerSetValid= as unique, membership-bounded signer identities;
- =RealizesQuorum= as signer validity plus both realized thresholds; and
- =AcceptsQuorumCertificate= as feasibility, digest equality, and realization.

The proof does not treat a signature as an arbitrary weight claim. Weight and
domain identity are projected from the committed policy. The explicit domain
witness also avoids hiding coverage behind a runtime de-duplication function:
the certificate says exactly which distinct domains justify its count.

* Proof obligations

The focused Lean module proves:

1. acceptance implies the realized weight threshold;
2. acceptance implies the realized failure-domain threshold;
3. insufficient realized weight rejects the certificate;
4. insufficient realized domain coverage rejects the certificate;
5. an unknown signer rejects the certificate;
6. a duplicate signer rejects the certificate; and
7. RFC 10.05.88 well-formedness does not imply policy feasibility.

* Consequence for SearchLoop

The rejoin path gains a strict verification order:

#+begin_example
canonical membership digest
  -> feasible membership policy
  -> unique membership-bounded signer set
  -> realized voting weight
  -> realized distinct-domain coverage
  -> certificate acceptance
#+end_example

This order reduces wasted search and verification work. An infeasible policy or
invalid signer identity terminates the path before the verifier explores
downstream evidence edges. It also keeps the agent-facing result compact: the
receipt needs to expose failed predicates and measured totals, not a complex
command surface.

* Proof-discovered next boundary

Quorum realization still does not prove quorum intersection. If the committed
weight threshold is too small relative to total voting weight, two different
certificates can each realize the policy while their signer sets are disjoint.
Failure-domain coverage alone does not repair that defect.

The next proof boundary must therefore model weighted quorum intersection and
state the threshold conditions under which independently accepted certificates
share sufficient committed voting authority.
