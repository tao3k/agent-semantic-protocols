#+title: RFC 10.05.84 — Activation Repair Transaction
#+subtitle: Independent recovery authority, linearizable candidate publication, and proof-gated evolution
#+language: en
#+options: toc:3 num:t

* Status

Draft for Lean-backed architectural audit.

This RFC specifies safety properties only. It does not claim repair liveness
without explicit scheduler, authority-service, and host-progress assumptions.

* Motivation

RFC 10.05.83 established that activation readiness must be a fresh derived
proof rather than a cached registry label. The next unresolved problem is more
fundamental: a failed activation cannot safely repair itself when recovery
authority, discovery, and execution all depend on the failed resident artifact.

The live development session supplied a concrete counterexample:

- parser-owned source access required resuming the resident =asp-explore= child;
- the authoritative resident registry returned an empty =sessions= set for the
  current root session;
- the hook nevertheless prescribed that absent child as the only recovery
  route;
- raw source fallback correctly remained forbidden.

The system therefore failed closed, but it had no authorized progress
transition. Safety without an independent repair path becomes permanent
unavailability.

* Design conclusion

Repair is not an imperative command such as “restart the resident.” It is a
proof-gated, compare-and-swap transaction over a canonical activation head.

A valid repair transaction binds all of the following:

1. an independently issued and revocable repair capability;
2. the canonical runtime identity;
3. the exact failed source generation;
4. the exact observed authority revision;
5. a strictly newer bounded candidate generation;
6. schema and binary identity for that candidate;
7. a compatibility witness;
8. a rollback checkpoint;
9. quarantine evidence for ambiguous dispatched work; and
10. a fresh readiness derivation after candidate publication.

The repair capability must not require the broken executable, missing resident
child, or stale registry record to validate or invoke it.

* Admission predicate

#+name: activation-repair-admission-model
#+begin_src typst
#let authorized = $not v
  and i_c = i
  and i_c = i_p
  and g_c = g
  and r_0 <= r
  and r <= r_1
  and g' <= g_max$

#let admissible = $p in {"degraded", "repairing"}
  and "activation-readable"
  and "independent-repair-capability"
  and "authorized"
  and g_p = g
  and r_p = r
  and g < g'
  and w_c
  and w_r
  and ("dispatch-recoverable" or w_q)
  and b' != epsilon$

$ "CanCommitRepair"(C, P, H)
  := "Authorized"(C, P, H) and "Admissible"(P, H) $

"ok"
#+end_src

This separates authorization from admissibility. Possession of a repair tool is
not sufficient authority, and an authority token does not erase runtime safety
obligations.

* Normative requirements

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ART-INDEPENDENCE

At least one emergency repair capability MUST be discoverable and verifiable
without executing the activation artifact, contacting the resident being
repaired, or trusting its cached readiness record.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ART-IDENTITY

A repair capability, proposal, and repair head MUST bind the same canonical
runtime identity. Display names and executable paths are insufficient identity
proofs.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ART-LEASE

A repair capability MUST bind issuance and expiry revisions and MUST support
revocation. Expired or revoked capabilities MUST fail closed.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ART-CAS

Candidate publication MUST compare both expected generation and expected
authority revision. A successful commit MUST increment the authority revision,
making every competing proposal for the previous head stale.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ART-GENERATION

The target generation MUST strictly exceed the failed source generation and
MUST NOT exceed the capability's authorized generation bound.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ART-DISPATCH

If dispatch evidence is =missingAfterDispatch=, repair MUST NOT publish a
candidate until the ambiguous work is terminally reconciled or quarantined
under an explicit policy.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ART-ROLLBACK

Candidate publication MUST carry both a compatibility witness and a rollback
checkpoint. A repair without either proof MUST be rejected before mutation.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ART-PUBLICATION

Commit publishes candidate intent and enters =activating=. It MUST NOT publish
=ready=. Readiness is derived later from fresh host, registry, transport,
dispatch, runtime-identity, and authority-revision observations.

* Linearization and concurrency

For a valid proposal at revision $r$, commit produces revision $r + 1$.

#+name: activation-repair-linearization-proposition
#+begin_src typst
$ "CanCommitRepair"(C, P, H_r)
  arrow.r.double
  "revision"("commit"(C, P, H_r)) = r + 1 $

$ r_P = r
  and "revision"("commit"(C, P, H_r)) = r + 1
  arrow.r.double
  not "CanCommitRepair"(C, P, H_(r + 1)) $

"ok"
#+end_src

This is a safety statement, not a distributed consensus claim. The authority
store still owns durable compare-and-swap linearization. Lean proves that the
protocol predicate rejects a losing stale proposal.

* Repair transaction

1. Observe the canonical repair head and authority revision.
2. Capture ambiguous dispatch evidence and quarantine if required.
3. Obtain an independent, unexpired, unrevoked repair capability.
4. Construct a candidate with a strict generation advance.
5. Attach compatibility and rollback witnesses.
6. Compare-and-swap the exact generation and authority revision.
7. Publish the candidate in =activating=, never =ready=.
8. Reacquire host, registry, transport, dispatch, runtime identity, and current
   authority observations.
9. Derive =TrustedReady= only from that fresh observation aggregate.
10. Retire the failed generation only after the rollback window closes.

* Lean audit

The executable model is:

- =packages/proofs/ASPProof/ActivationRepairTransaction.lean=

The model proves:

- a valid base repair is committable;
- expired, revoked, or cross-identity capabilities are rejected;
- stale-revision and generation-regression proposals are rejected;
- rollback evidence is mandatory;
- ambiguous dispatch requires reconciliation or quarantine;
- commit advances generation and authority revision;
- commit preserves canonical identity;
- commit returns to =activating=; and
- the winning commit makes the same proposal stale at the new head.

* Logic bugs found

** Capability presence is not capability independence

RFC 10.05.83 required a repair tool or emergency capability, but did not prove
that the capability remains reachable when the activated artifact and resident
child are absent. This RFC strengthens the requirement: emergency repair
authority must be validated and invoked outside the failed activation closure.

** Generation-only CAS admits ABA-like authority drift

Two observations can share a generation while differing in registry binding,
revocation state, or dispatch reconciliation. Generation equality alone is not
a sufficient compare-and-swap coordinate. The authority revision is therefore
part of every proposal.

** Successful repair is not successful activation

Publishing a candidate proves only that one repair proposal won. It does not
prove host reachability, transport binding, dispatch recovery, or runtime
identity. Commit deliberately invalidates readiness and returns to
=activating=.

** Quarantine is a policy boundary

Quarantine prevents an ambiguous dispatch from blocking all repair forever,
but may duplicate external effects if policy is unsound. Lean can prove that a
quarantine witness is required; it cannot prove the external policy safe
without an effect model.

* Explicit liveness boundary

The following claims are not established by this RFC:

- an authority service will issue a capability;
- a scheduler will execute a winning proposal;
- the host will become reachable;
- candidate publication will eventually be observed;
- an external side effect can always be rolled back; or
- a quarantined dispatch will eventually become terminal.

Future liveness work must introduce a transition system plus explicit fairness,
bounded-failure, and authority-availability assumptions. These assumptions must
not be hidden inside a safety theorem named “repair succeeds.”

* Next proof frontier

The next RFC should model repair liveness under bounded environmental failure:

- authority capability renewal and revocation races;
- durable candidate-publication outbox semantics;
- host observation leases and freshness expiry;
- rollback-window closure;
- retirement and garbage collection of failed generations; and
- eventual reconciliation of quarantined dispatches.
