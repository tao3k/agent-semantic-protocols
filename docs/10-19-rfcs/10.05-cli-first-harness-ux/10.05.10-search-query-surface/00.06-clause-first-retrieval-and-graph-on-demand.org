#+TITLE: Clause-first Retrieval and Graph-on-Demand
#+OPTIONS: toc:3

* Status
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-CFR-GOD-STATUS
:END:

This module records the design decision and its constraints before any Python,
Rust, or Lean implementation.  It refines SearchLoop retrieval; it does not
replace the evidence obligation, exact query, graph projection, or closure
contracts.

* Problem
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-CFR-GOD-PROBLEM
:END:

Always materializing and ranking a graph is too expensive for selective,
anchored, shallow searches.  Replacing the graph with unqualified triples would
be faster but logically weaker: code relations are role-sensitive, qualified,
provenance-bound, and often n-ary.

The design problem is therefore not “triple store or graph.”  It is how to use
factorized relation indexes and partially bound logical clauses on the online
hot path while preserving the evidence and closure semantics that require a
graph or exact materialization.

* Decision
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-CFR-GOD-DECISION
:END:

ASP adopts =Clause-first Retrieval + Graph-on-Demand=:

1. intent and contract clauses compile into typed evidence obligations;
2. obligations lower into qualified =AtomicEvidenceClause= values;
3. clauses with admitted bindings perform direct relation-posting lookups;
4. returned facts create new bindings and clause-state deltas;
5. a global planner selects the next bounded action;
6. ambiguous, completeness-sensitive, contradictory, or join-heavy frontiers
   escalate to an admitted =EvidenceGraph= projection;
7. exact source is materialized only from parser-owned selectors;
8. an independent closure validator decides terminal success.

The hot path is lighter than full graph construction, but it is not a separate
truth system.

* Three representations, not one
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-CFR-GOD-REPRESENTATIONS
:END:

** Physical relation postings

Storage indexes qualified fact identifiers by predicate, argument role, bound
entity, context, and source generation.  Postings optimize forward and inverse
lookup.  They are not the logical fact by themselves.

** Logical bound evidence clauses

The online planner reasons over role-labeled arguments, variables,
qualifications, scope, provenance requirements, freshness, and obligation
identity.  This is more expressive than a bare subject-predicate-object triple.

** Admitted evidence graph

The graph joins admitted facts and their provenance into the representation
needed for route reasoning, multi-hop relations, contradictions, enumeration,
absence, and closure-sensitive projection.

No representation may silently erase information required by the next.

* Online algorithm
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-CFR-GOD-ONLINE-ALGORITHM
:END:

For one SearchLoop continuation:

1. validate workspace, project, source snapshot, provider contracts, policy,
   and continuation identity;
2. compile unresolved obligations into clauses;
3. classify every clause as =resolved=, =searchable=, =fuzzy=, or =stalled=;
4. invalidate searched digests whose dependency identity changed;
5. identify newly searchable clauses;
6. produce bounded posting actions for admitted providers;
7. allocate a global candidate budget with fairness floors for mandatory
   obligations;
8. execute independent actions in serial, batch, or parallel form;
9. admit returned facts only after provenance and freshness checks;
10. extend bindings and reclassify affected clauses;
11. detect contradiction, cycle, ambiguity, join, enumeration, or absence
    conditions requiring graph escalation;
12. project a canonical delta and typed next-action proposals;
13. run closure validation when all required obligations appear discharged;
14. return a terminal receipt or the next bounded frontier.

The model-facing packet omits unchanged clauses, previously accepted evidence,
and raw postings.

* Online applicability
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-CFR-GOD-APPLICABILITY
:END:

Clause-first retrieval is preferred when:

- at least one canonical owner, entity, selector, or relation argument is bound;
- the required relation is provider-declared and posting-addressable;
- the query is local or shallow;
- candidate degree is bounded;
- no complete enumeration or absence proof is required;
- joins remain within a compatible identity and namespace;
- freshness can be checked without graph-wide reconstruction.

It is an optimization choice, not a semantic weakening.

* Mandatory graph escalation
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-CFR-GOD-ESCALATION
:END:

SearchLoop MUST escalate when any of these conditions holds:

- an enumeration or absence obligation lacks a closed admitted scope;
- a required join spans independent owners, namespaces, providers, or source
  generations;
- relation degree, unresolved variable count, or hop depth exceeds the bounded
  clause plan;
- clauses form a dependency cycle without a decreasing measure;
- admitted facts contradict each other;
- provider capabilities cannot preserve required argument roles or qualifiers;
- selector relocation or alias resolution is ambiguous;
- completeness-sensitive policy or test reachability is requested;
- the planner cannot justify that a local alternative is sufficient;
- exact-source verification contradicts a relation fact.

Cost and rank may recommend earlier escalation.  They cannot suppress a
mandatory escalation.

* EvidenceGraph authority refinement
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-CFR-GOD-GRAPH-AUTHORITY
:END:

The EvidenceGraph is the authoritative admitted evidence representation for
graph-dependent reasoning.  It is not self-proving truth.  Closure authority
belongs to the validator operating over:

- the obligation graph;
- admitted evidence and graph facts;
- exact selector materializations where required;
- freshness, provenance, contradiction, and scope proofs;
- the current RFC, schema, provider, and policy identities.

This distinction prevents both postings and graph rank from becoming an
unchecked success oracle.

* Global candidate budgeting
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-CFR-GOD-GLOBAL-BUDGET
:END:

Per-clause fixed top-k is rejected.  It can spend most of the budget on a
high-degree weak clause while starving a low-ranked mandatory obligation.

The global pool accounts for:

- obligation criticality;
- expected information gain;
- owner and selector diversity;
- provider dispatches;
- projected evidence and model-facing bytes;
- materialization and graph-escalation cost;
- prior attempts and searched digests;
- fairness floors for every mandatory searchable obligation.

Marginal utility orders allowed actions.  It never changes which obligations
are mandatory or which evidence is sufficient.  The planner reserves mandatory
floors before allocating optional utility budget.  If the available budget
cannot satisfy the declared minimum floors, it returns a typed
=budget-exhausted= or =needs-budget= state with the unserved obligations; it
does not assign a silent zero share.

* Continuation and once-only search
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-CFR-GOD-CONTINUATION
:END:

A clause action is “searched once” only relative to a validation key containing:

- clause semantic digest;
- bound argument and qualifier digest;
- workspace, project, and source generation;
- provider and parser artifact identity;
- query-pack and shared-schema identity;
- policy overlay;
- relevant budget and projection class.

Changing any dependency creates a new admissible search action.  A stable
digest prevents redundant rescans; it does not suppress fresh evidence.

* Parallel execution and canonical projection
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-CFR-GOD-PARALLELISM
:END:

Independent posting actions may execute concurrently.  Independence requires
compatible identity domains and no read-after-write dependency between their
bindings.

Runtime completion order is not semantic order.  Receipts use stable plan,
obligation, clause, and branch identities and a total canonical projection
order.  Conflicting or generation-incompatible results produce an explicit
conflict transition.

* Failure semantics
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-CFR-GOD-FAILURES
:END:

The online path returns typed states for:

- =no-bound-anchor=;
- =unsupported-relation=;
- =provider-capability-gap=;
- =binding-budget-exhausted=;
- =stale-clause=;
- =ambiguous-owner=;
- =join-required=;
- =graph-required=;
- =closed-scope-required=;
- =contradictory-evidence=;
- =no-sound-plan=.

None of these is equivalent to “fact absent.”

* Cache integration
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-CFR-GOD-CACHE
:END:

Clause classification, posting actions, branch evidence, graph projection, and
terminal receipts may be cached at separate layers.  Every entry is
witness-bound.  A cached clause resolution cannot survive a changed source
snapshot merely because its text is unchanged.

The semantic-cache contract is defined in
[[file:00.04-semantic-cache-hierarchy.org][Semantic cache hierarchy]].

* Improvement integration
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-CFR-GOD-IMPROVEMENT
:END:

Improvement state may learn branch ordering, cost estimates, graph-escalation
hints, and cache admission policy.  It cannot change clause meaning,
sufficiency, mandatory escalation, closure, or provider authority.

Policy evaluation optimizes independently validated closure and downstream
task success, not easy-clause hit rate.

* Compatibility and migration
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-CFR-GOD-MIGRATION
:END:

The migration is additive:

1. define shared clause, posting, escalation, and receipt schemas;
2. generate clause plans alongside the existing graph path;
3. run shadow comparison without controlling retrieval;
4. enable bounded anchored relation classes;
5. add branch-cache and deterministic transcript receipts;
6. enable graph-on-demand only for scenario classes with equal evidence
   closure;
7. retain direct graph execution as a typed recovery and conformance path;
8. widen relation classes only after provider alignment and Lean obligations.

Existing graph, router, selector, and closure machinery is not discarded.

* Performance and quality gates
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-CFR-GOD-GATES
:END:

Receipts compare clause-first and graph-first execution on identical intents:

- provider, graph, exact-query, and model call count;
- unique owners, selectors, facts, and clause actions;
- candidate and model-facing bytes;
- clause rescans and cache validated-hit rate;
- graph escalation count and reason;
- cold, warm, and invalidation latency;
- closure-validator outcome;
- missing, stale, contradictory, or incorrectly accepted evidence;
- deterministic transcript digest;
- downstream task correctness.

Lower latency is not accepted when closure quality or fail-closed behavior
regresses.

* Non-goals
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-CFR-GOD-NON-GOALS
:END:

- replacing qualified relations with bare triples;
- proving absence from ranked or truncated postings;
- using graph rank as closure;
- removing exact parser-owned query;
- making Python planner output semantic authority;
- requiring concurrency for correctness;
- hiding provider asymmetry behind silent fallback;
- changing RFC semantics through learned policy.
