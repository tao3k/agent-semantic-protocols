#+title: SearchRoute Cache Rejoin Quorum Intersection
#+subtitle: From independently realized certificates to fault-bounded common voting authority
#+options: toc:3 num:t

* Status

This document is a proof-backed refinement of RFC 10.05.89. It specifies Lean
semantics and executable Org-Babel Typst mathematics only. It does not
authorize Rust or Python implementation.

* Problem

RFC 10.05.89 proves that each accepted certificate independently:

- binds to a feasible canonical membership policy;
- uses unique, membership-bounded signer identities;
- reaches the committed voting-weight threshold; and
- reaches the committed failure-domain threshold.

Independent realization is not agreement safety. Two signer sets can each
reach the threshold while sharing no voting authority. Even when strict
majority forces a positive intersection, all intersecting weight can still be
faulty.

The verifier and graph router therefore need a pairwise proof boundary. They
must not infer common honest authority merely from two successful
single-certificate receipts.

* Normative clauses

** CRQI-ACCOUNTING: pairwise weight accounting

For two signer sets under one canonical membership policy, pairwise accounting
MUST expose:

- the realized weight of each signer set;
- the total committed membership weight;
- the weight of their signer intersection; and
- an inclusion-exclusion bound stating that the sum of the two realized
  weights is at most total weight plus intersection weight.

The intersection value is voting weight, not signer count.

** CRQI-PAIR: accepted-pair lower bound

If both certificates are accepted under the same membership policy, their
realized weights are each at least the committed quorum threshold. Combined
with CRQI-ACCOUNTING, this implies that twice the quorum threshold is at most
total weight plus intersection weight.

** CRQI-MAJORITY: positive intersection

If total committed weight is strictly less than twice the quorum threshold,
two accepted certificates have positive intersection weight.

Equality is insufficient. A total weight of 2 with quorum threshold 1 permits
two disjoint weight-1 quorums.

** CRQI-HONEST: fault-bounded intersection

Let =B= denote the maximum faulty voting weight. To establish that the
intersection contains more weight than the entire fault budget, the verifier
MUST require:

#+begin_example
totalWeight + B < quorumWeight + quorumWeight
#+end_example

Under CRQI-PAIR this implies:

#+begin_example
B < intersectionWeight
#+end_example

If actual faulty weight is bounded by =B=, the common intersection cannot be
entirely faulty.

** CRQI-COUNTEREXAMPLE: reject weaker implications

The protocol MUST NOT encode either of these invalid implications:

1. two independently realized quorums always intersect; or
2. positive quorum intersection always contains honest authority.

The Lean model contains a counterexample for each implication.

** CRQI-ROUTER: graph routing consequence

When the SearchLoop compares competing rejoin certificates, the graph router
MUST route through pairwise intersection evidence before treating the
certificates as agreement-compatible.

The shortest valid proof path is:

#+begin_example
accepted left certificate
  + accepted right certificate
  + shared canonical membership
  + pairwise weight accounting
  + fault-bound threshold
  -> common honest voting authority
#+end_example

Two independent acceptance nodes without this pairwise edge are not sufficient
evidence.

* Executable mathematical model

The prose uses =T= for total committed weight, =Q= for quorum weight, =I= for
intersection weight, and =B= for maximum faulty weight. Formal mathematical
content is owned by Org-Babel Typst.

#+name: searchroute-cache-rejoin-quorum-intersection-model
#+begin_src typst
#let accounting(left, right, total, intersection) = (
  left + right <= total + intersection
)
#let both-realize(quorum, left, right) = (
  quorum <= left and quorum <= right
)
#let positive-overlap-threshold(total, quorum) = (
  total < quorum + quorum
)
#let honest-overlap-threshold(total, fault-bound, quorum) = (
  total + fault-bound < quorum + quorum

#assert(accounting(2, 2, 3, 1))
#assert(both-realize(2, 2, 2))
#assert(positive-overlap-threshold(3, 2))
#assert(not honest-overlap-threshold(3, 1, 2))

#assert(both-realize(1, 1, 1))

* Lean model

The proof owner is:

#+begin_example
packages/proofs/ASPProof/SearchRouteAdmissionRetryCacheRejoinQuorumIntersection.lean
#+end_example

=PairWeightAccounting= binds a pair of signer sets to an intersection weight
and the inclusion-exclusion bound. It deliberately consumes the concrete
realized-weight functions from RFC 10.05.89 instead of accepting arbitrary
certificate-reported weights.

The focused module proves:

1. two accepted certificates establish the overlap lower bound;
2. the fault-aware threshold forces intersection weight above the fault bound;
3. strict weighted majority forces positive intersection;
4. a half-total threshold admits two disjoint realized quorums; and
5. strict-majority overlap can still be entirely faulty.

* Search and token-cost consequence

Pairwise intersection is a graph edge, not another complex CLI command. The
agent should receive a compact receipt containing:

- the two certificate identities;
- canonical membership identity;
- =T=, =Q=, =B=, and =I=;
- the inclusion-exclusion verdict;
- the honest-overlap threshold verdict; and
- the next failed predicate, if any.

This avoids repeated traversal of the two complete signer subgraphs. A valid
pairwise receipt becomes a short reusable route from competing certificates to
their common-authority conclusion. Search-result caching may reuse that receipt
only while both certificate identities, membership identity, fault bound, and
accounting inputs remain unchanged. Model prefix caching is a separate
optimization and MUST NOT substitute for evidence identity.

* Proof-discovered next boundary

The current Lean theorem assumes =PairWeightAccounting.inclusionExclusionBound=
as evidence. It does not yet derive that bound from the concrete canonical
membership list and the two signer sets.

The next proof boundary must construct intersection weight from member identity,
prove that duplicates cannot inflate it, and derive inclusion-exclusion from
the finite committed membership itself. Until then, an implementation must not
treat an unaudited externally supplied accounting value as authoritative.
