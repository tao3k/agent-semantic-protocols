#+title: RFC 10.05.71 — SearchRoute Distributed Cache Invalidation Barrier
#+author: ASP Search Protocol Working Notes
#+startup: overview

* Status

This RFC is a proof-oriented refinement of RFC 10.05.70.  It defines the
distributed barrier required before cache replicas may continue serving after
authorization-generation invalidation.  It does not implement a distributed
cache in Rust or Python.

* Abstract

Emitting an invalidation event does not prove that every cache replica has
installed it.  A quorum acknowledgment is also insufficient when an
unacknowledged replica remains eligible to serve reads.

The safety condition quantifies over the serving set:

#+begin_example
for every replica r:
  if r may serve,
  then r acknowledged
  and targetGeneration <= r.installedGeneration
#+end_example

An unacknowledged replica may coexist with a completed quorum only when it is
fenced from serving.  This permits availability-oriented quorum protocols
without treating a late replica as current.

* Normative requirements

** ASP-RFC-10.05-DCIB-TARGET

Every invalidation barrier MUST bind the target authorization generation.
Wall-clock event time MUST NOT substitute for generation identity.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-DCIB-SERVING-SET

Barrier safety MUST quantify every replica currently eligible to serve winner
cache reads.  Registry membership, health status, routing eligibility, and
fencing state MUST determine one authoritative serving set.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-DCIB-ACKNOWLEDGMENT

Every serving replica MUST acknowledge the invalidation and report an installed
generation greater than or equal to the target.

Event emission, queue acceptance, delivery attempt, or local receipt parsing
is not an installed-generation acknowledgment.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-DCIB-QUORUM

A quorum acknowledgment is sufficient only when every unacknowledged replica
is fenced from serving before the barrier is declared safe.

Quorum size alone is not the safety predicate.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-DCIB-STALE-ENTRY

At a serving replica that crossed the barrier, an entry older than the target
generation MUST NOT be considered current.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-DCIB-NONIMPLICATION

The protocol MUST preserve these explicit non-implications:

- invalidation-event emission does not imply barrier completion;
- one delivery acknowledgment does not imply replica installation;
- quorum acknowledgment does not protect an unfenced late replica;
- replica health does not imply installed-generation freshness;
- local cache equality does not imply global authorization currency;
- distributed invalidation safety does not prove graph-route optimality.

* Mathematical model

The Typst source remains embedded in Org and is evaluated through stdin.

#+name: searchroute-distributed-cache-invalidation-barrier-model
#+begin_src typst
#let serving(replica) = $upright("Serving")(#replica)$
#let acknowledged(replica) = $upright("Acknowledged")(#replica)$
#let installed(replica) = $upright("InstalledGeneration")(#replica)$
#let target = $upright("TargetGeneration")$
#let safe = $upright("BarrierSafe")$
#let fenced(replica) = $upright("Fenced")(#replica)$

#[
  Distributed barrier safety is
  $
    #safe
    equiv
    (
      forall r,
      #serving($r$)
      arrow.r.double
      (
        #acknowledged($r$)
        and #target <= #installed($r$)
      )
    ).
  $

  Therefore a serving replica that crossed the barrier cannot treat an entry
  generation $e < #target$ as current.

  Event emission alone does not entail $#safe$.  A quorum acknowledgment also
  does not entail $#safe$ while an unacknowledged replica remains serving.

  Quorum completion becomes safe when every late replica satisfies
  $
    not #acknowledged($r$)
    arrow.r.double
    #fenced($r$)
    and not #serving($r$).
  $
]
#+end_src

* SearchLoop cost relevance

A late cache replica can reintroduce revoked winner evidence, causing unsafe
route reuse.  Overly conservative global cache shutdown instead increases
searches, tool rounds, tokens, and model-prefix disruption.  Serving-set
fencing permits safe quorum-based progress without accepting stale reads.

* Non-goals and next proof boundary

This RFC does not prove failure-detector accuracy, fencing-token authenticity,
replicated serving-set linearizability, or graph-route optimality.

The next proof layer should bind serving eligibility to a monotonic fencing
token and prove that a partitioned replica cannot resume serving with a stale
token after rejoining.
