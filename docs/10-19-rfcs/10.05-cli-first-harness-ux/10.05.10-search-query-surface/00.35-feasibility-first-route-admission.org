#+title: RFC 10.05.10.00.35 — Feasibility-First Route Admission
#+startup: overview

* Status

Draft research specification.

This RFC refines route selection over the canonical graph-cost vector. It does
not add CLI commands or authorize a runtime implementation.

* Problem

RFC 00.32 defines a canonical cost vector ordered by graph hops, uncached
tokens, rounds, and transitions. A hop-first lexicographic order is useful for
comparing executable routes, but it is unsafe as the admission rule.

Consider:

| Route | Graph hops | Tokens | Rounds | Transitions |
|-------+------------+--------+--------+-------------|
| A     |          1 |    100 |      1 |           1 |
| B     |          2 |     10 |      1 |           1 |

Under hop-first lexicographic order, A precedes B. Under a 20-token budget, A
cannot execute and B can. Therefore:

#+begin_quote
lexicographically shortest does not imply feasible.
#+end_quote

Optimizing the graph before applying budgets can select a route that cannot
realize its evidence claim.

* Decision

The Graph Router MUST use a two-stage relation:

1. admission: reject candidates that are not feasible under the active route
   budget and runtime capability premises;
2. optimization: compare only admitted candidates using the canonical graph
   cost relation.

Feasibility is not another weighted cost dimension. A budget violation is a
hard boundary. No improvement in graph hops can compensate for exceeding the
token, round, or transition budget.

* Route budget

The proof model uses an explicit route budget:

- maximum graph hops;
- maximum uncached tokens under the active cost context;
- maximum model/tool rounds; and
- maximum router transitions.

The token field is interpreted only after the cache-parametric and ex-ante
authority checks from RFC 00.33 and RFC 00.34. A stale hit certificate cannot
make an otherwise infeasible route admissible.

A future runtime can add latency, money, memory, or provider quotas through a
new versioned budget contract. It MUST NOT silently reinterpret one of the
existing dimensions.

* Admitted comparison

=AdmittedNoWorse budget left right= means:

- =left= is feasible; and
- either =right= is infeasible or =left= is no worse than =right= under the
  canonical cost relation.

The relation intentionally does not require an infeasible right-hand candidate
to have a meaningful total order. It is outside the optimization domain.

This gives the router a direct proof obligation:

#+begin_quote
Every selected route is feasible, and every feasible competitor is no better.
#+end_quote

* Omitted frontier

An omitted candidate family can be closed in one of two ways:

1. an infeasibility certificate proves that every member violates a hard
   admission premise; or
2. a cost lower-bound certificate proves that an admitted visible route is no
   worse than every feasible member.

An omitted frontier MUST NOT be closed merely because its minimum graph-hop
count exceeds the chosen route. A longer path may be the only feasible path.

For symbolic frontier bounds, feasibility and cost bounds remain separate:

- lower bounds can prove unavoidable budget violation;
- cost bounds rank the residual feasible set.

* SearchLoop behavior

SearchLoop remains interactive:

1. inspect the current evidence graph and cost context;
2. admit feasible candidate families;
3. request a focused refinement only when feasibility is unknown;
4. optimize the admitted set;
5. execute or return a bounded failure receipt.

Unknown feasibility is not treated as =false= or =true=. It produces a next
action that asks for the missing budget, capability, lease, or evidence fact.
That preserves fail-closed behavior without forcing a complex CLI surface.

* Python Turbo Graph consequence

The future Python graph engine SHOULD maintain separate indexes for:

- structural reachability lower bounds;
- resource-feasibility lower bounds; and
- cost ranking among admitted candidates.

This separation avoids expanding the entire graph to discover that the
shortest structural route exceeds a hard budget. It also prevents a graph-hop
heuristic from hiding the only executable route.

Candidate generation MAY use aggressive heuristics. Route certification MUST
retain the two-stage admission/optimization semantics.

* Required Lean obligations

The companion Lean model MUST prove:

- admitted comparison implies left-side feasibility;
- the short one-hop example is lexicographically preferred;
- the short example violates the token budget;
- the longer example satisfies every budget dimension; and
- the combined counterexample demonstrates that hop-first preference does not
  imply feasibility.

The modular Lean audit emits these theorem families as JSON.

* Design finding

The earlier graph-cost model conflated two questions:

- which route has the smallest canonical cost?
- which route is executable under current constraints?

Lean separates these propositions. No theorem can derive =Feasible= from the
lexicographic relation alone, and the concrete counterexample makes the missing
premise executable. The RFC therefore changes route selection from
=minimize(all candidates)= to =minimize(filter feasible candidates)=.
