:PROPERTIES:

This dossier preserves the reasoning behind the Clause-first decision.  It
prevents a short architecture summary from erasing assumptions, rejected
alternatives, unresolved questions, or counterexamples.

* Rejected or narrowed alternatives
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-CFR-ALTERNATIVES
:END:

** Full graph on every search

Rejected as the default because selective anchored queries pay graph
materialization and projection costs before demonstrating need.  Retained as a
conformance, mandatory-escalation, and recovery path.

** Bare triple store as the logical model

Rejected because relation roles, arity, qualifiers, provenance, scope,
snapshot, and provider authority are proof-relevant.

** Relation postings as closure evidence

Rejected.  Postings retrieve candidate logical facts.  Admission and closure
remain separate.

** Rank or model confidence as closure

Rejected.  Rank orders allowed actions; it neither establishes completeness nor
discharges an obligation.

** Fixed top-k per clause

Rejected because it ignores a shared budget and can starve mandatory but
low-ranked obligations.  Replaced by global selection with fairness floors.

** Model-generated variable binding

Rejected for canonical owners and selectors.  Models may propose a search
intent; admitted bindings come from providers, catalogs, graphs, or exact
queries.

** Cost-only graph threshold

Rejected because providers or learned policies can game estimates and because
some escalation conditions are proof-mandatory.

** Automatically learned normative semantics

Rejected.  Learned policy selects among allowed actions.  Normative change
enters the RFC and Lean repair loop.

* Open design questions
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-CFR-OPEN-QUESTIONS
:END:

- Which relation classes have provider-independent shared semantics?
- Which qualifiers are mandatory for each shared relation kind?
- Which clauses can be proven locally without an EvidenceGraph projection?
- What finite scope witnesses are available for enumeration and absence?
- How are graph-escalation bounds calibrated without becoming semantic rules?
- What fairness algorithm protects mandatory obligations while remaining
  globally cost-aware?
- Which joins can storage prove complete?
- How is contradiction represented when providers have different authority?
- What selector relocation proof is sufficient across snapshots?
- Which cache dependencies are minimal but safe?
- Which shadow scenarios establish equivalence with graph-first execution?
- How is downstream task success attributed to a planning policy without reward
  leakage?
- Which assumptions belong to the environment and which must be RFC theorems?

These questions remain explicit.  Implementations may experiment behind shadow
receipts but cannot silently choose normative answers.

* Decision revision protocol
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-CFR-REVISION
:END:

Every revision to a CFR claim:

1. names the claim and affected risk identities;
2. states new assumptions;
3. updates its normative owner;
4. changes the AXLE declaration bundle digest;
5. invalidates dependent Lean receipts;
6. replays the associated minimal witnesses;
7. records the new acceptance evidence.
