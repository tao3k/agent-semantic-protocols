* Abstract

=00.16= proved constrained route selection over a finite candidate set, but
its =hopCount= was the length of the SearchLoop step list. That quantity is a
transition count, not necessarily the number of graph edges traversed.

This distinction is normative:

- =graphHops= measures semantic graph-edge distance from source to target;
- =transitionCount= measures bounded SearchLoop actions;
- =roundCost= measures agent/LLM observation and decision boundaries.

A router jump may reduce transitions without selecting a shorter graph path.
Lean now contains an executable counterexample where the old transition-first
router selects =graphHops=5 over =graphHops=2=. The corrected graph router
orders feasible candidates by:

1. graph-edge hops;
2. uncached model tokens;
3. LLM rounds;
4. SearchLoop transitions.

The RFC also establishes a conditional global theorem:

#+begin_quote
If candidate generation is complete for every validated feasible walk in the
active ranked DAG, then a locally optimal generated candidate itself realizes
the source-to-target walk and is globally no worse than every validated
feasible graph route.
#+end_quote

The completeness certificate is still an assumption. This RFC deliberately
does not claim that current providers already produce it.

* Defect Found in the Previous Cost Model

The previous definition was:

#+begin_src lean
def hopCount (route : SearchRoute) : Nat :=
  route.steps.length
#+end_src

The definition is computationally valid, but its name allowed two different
interpretations:

- number of SearchLoop transitions;
- number of semantic graph edges.

Those interpretations are not equivalent. A single router transition can
cross a direct typed graph edge, dispatch a bounded graph query, or merely
compress several command-oriented navigation steps. Conversely, a route with
more SearchLoop transitions may follow a much shorter graph path.

The Lean refinement therefore keeps the old value as =transitionCount= and
introduces an independent =GraphCandidate.graphHops= field.

* Normative Clauses

** ASP-RFC-10.05-SRD-SEPARATE-HOPS

Every graph-route receipt distinguishes:

- =graphHops=: validated semantic graph edges from source to target;
- =transitionCount=: SearchLoop route-step length;
- =roundCost=: model decision boundaries.

No renderer, planner, or audit may label transition count as graph distance.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-SRD-RANKED-DAG

The proof graph carries:

- a finite node type;
- a typed directed edge predicate;
- a natural-number rank for each node;
- a maximum rank;
- proof that every edge strictly raises rank;
- proof that every node rank is bounded by the maximum rank.

For a walk from =source= to =target= with =h= graph hops, Lean proves:

- =rank(source) + h <= rank(target)=;
- a walk from a node back to itself has =h=0;
- =h + rank(source) <= maxRank=.

These properties make positive cycles impossible and establish a graph-hop
bound independently of SearchLoop transitions.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-SRD-PATH-VALIDATION

A short =graphHops= claim is not feasible unless its path receipt is
validated. The receipt must eventually bind:

- graph snapshot identity;
- source and target identities;
- ordered typed edge identities;
- graph-hop count;
- provider artifact and schema identity;
- validation domain and closure obligation.

The current Lean model represents this boundary as =pathValidated=. That
Boolean is a proof abstraction, not a final wire format.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-SRD-CANDIDATE-COMPLETE

For graph =G=, source =s=, target =t=, requirement =q=, and budget =B=,
candidate generation is complete exactly when every candidate that:

- realizes a ranked-DAG walk from =s= to =t=;
- has a validated path;
- satisfies route admissibility;
- satisfies graph-hop and route budgets;

is present in the generated finite candidate list.

Completeness is stronger than parser correctness and stronger than returning
one plausible route.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-SRD-GLOBAL-OPTIMALITY

Let =C= be a complete candidate list and =r*= be locally optimal over =C=.
The local certificate must prove that =r*= itself realizes a walk from =s= to
=t=. Then =r*= is globally no worse than every validated feasible candidate
that realizes such a walk.

The theorem is conditional on completeness. Absence of a completeness receipt
limits the claim to local finite-candidate optimality.

= SearchRoute ranked DAG and conditional global optimality

== Three independent distance measures

For graph candidate $c$, define

$ G(c) = "validated graph-edge hops", $

$ N(c) = "SearchLoop transitions", $

$ R(c) = "LLM observation and decision rounds", $

$ U(c) = "uncached model tokens". $

In general,

$ G(c) != N(c), quad N(c) != R(c), quad G(c) != R(c). $

A router transition may compress command interactions without reducing graph
distance. A model-prefix hit may reduce $U$ without changing any of
$G$, $N$, or $R$.

== Ranked DAG

Let

$ cal(G) = (V, E, lambda, M), $

where $V$ is finite, $E subset.eq V times V$, $lambda: V -> NN$ is a rank,
and $M$ is a maximum rank.

The ranked-DAG obligations are

$ (u,v) in E => lambda(u) < lambda(v), $

$ "and for every" v in V, quad lambda(v) <= M. $

For a walk

$ w: s arrow.r t $

with $h$ graph edges, Lean proves

$ lambda(s) + h <= lambda(t). $

Therefore a positive cycle is impossible:

$ w: v arrow.r v => h = 0. $

The maximum-rank bound yields

$ h + lambda(s) <= M. $

Graph-hop boundedness follows from graph structure, not from a SearchLoop
round budget.

== Graph candidate and feasibility

A graph candidate is

$ c = (r, h, v_p), $

where $r$ is a SearchRoute, $h=G(c)$, and $v_p$ is the path-validation
receipt.

For route requirement $q$ and graph budget

$ B_G = (B_"route", B_h, B_n), $

define

$ F_G(q,B_G,c) =
  v_p
  and h <= B_h
  and N(c) <= B_n
  and F_"route"(q,B_"route",r). $

An unvalidated one-hop claim is not a feasible shortcut.

== Corrected routing order

The graph-aware key is

$ K_G(c) = (G(c), U(c), R(c), N(c)). $

For feasible candidates, comparison is lexicographic:

1. minimum graph-edge hops;
2. minimum uncached model tokens;
3. minimum model rounds;
4. minimum SearchLoop transitions.

Budgets still constrain raw tokens, evidence tokens, uncached tokens, rounds,
and graph hops before this comparison.

== Transition-minimization counterexample

Let

$ c_a = (G=5, N=3), $

$ c_b = (G=2, N=5). $

A transition-first selector chooses $c_a$ because $3 < 5$. A graph-first
selector chooses $c_b$ because $2 < 5$.

Thus

$ arg min N(c) quad != quad arg min G(c). $

The old selector cannot establish shortest graph-path semantics.

== Candidate completeness

Let $cal(C)$ be the finite generated candidate set. Define

$ "Complete"(cal(G),s,t,q,B_G,cal(C)) $

iff every candidate $c$ satisfying

$ "Walk"(cal(G),s,t,G(c)) $

and

$ F_G(q,B_G,c) $

also satisfies

$ c in cal(C). $

This is a coverage statement. It is not implied by successful parsing, one
no-hit response, or one locally best candidate.

== Local-to-global theorem

Suppose selected candidate $c^*$ is locally optimal:

$ "Walk"(cal(G),s,t,G(c^*)), $

$ c^* in cal(C), $

$ F_G(q,B_G,c^*), $

$ "and for every" c in cal(C),
  F_G(q,B_G,c) => K_G(c^*) <=_"lex" K_G(c). $

If candidate generation is complete, then for every globally realizable
validated feasible candidate $x$,

$ "Walk"(cal(G),s,t,G(x))
  and F_G(q,B_G,x)
  => K_G(c^*) <=_"lex" K_G(x). $

The proof is direct: completeness provides $x in cal(C)$, after which local
optimality applies. The selected-walk premise separately prevents an unrelated
but low-cost generated item from being reported as a global route optimum.

== What the theorem does not provide

The ranked-DAG theorem bounds walk length, but it does not by itself generate
all SearchRoute variants. Route costs, cache identities, provider choices,
and materialization choices add dimensions not determined solely by graph
nodes.

Therefore the remaining implementation certificate must prove both:

- graph-walk enumeration completeness;
- route-realization completeness for each enumerated walk.

Until both are available, the correct claim is conditional global optimality.

RFC 10.05.18 constructs and verifies the fuel-bounded graph-walk enumerator.
It also proves that token and round budgets do not imply a finite route catalog:
arbitrarily many zero-cost transitions may be inserted without changing those
costs. Consequently, =GraphRouteBudget= now includes an independent
=maxTransitions= bound. RFC 10.05.18 constructs the bounded route catalog from
a budget-derived finite step-instance catalog and proves bounded candidate
completeness without an external catalog-coverage premise.

See [[file:00.18-searchroute-constructive-enumeration.org][Constructive SearchRoute enumeration]].

* Logic Defects Exposed by This Refinement

1. SearchLoop step length was incorrectly available as a graph-distance
   interpretation.
2. The previous route budget had no independent graph-hop ceiling.
3. A path with fewer transitions can have more graph edges.
4. A claimed one-hop path needs a graph-snapshot-bound path receipt.
5. Finite graph nodes and bounded walk length do not automatically make the
   space of SearchRoute cost/cache/provider realizations complete.
6. Global optimality is invalid without a candidate-completeness certificate.
7. A local optimum must itself realize the requested source-to-target walk;
   membership and low cost alone are insufficient.

* Remaining Research

** Constructive candidate enumeration

Define a finite graph-walk enumerator and prove soundness, uniqueness policy,
and completeness under the ranked-DAG bound.

** Route realization

For each graph walk, enumerate or otherwise bound provider, cache,
materialization, and projection variants that can affect the routing key.

** Receipt binding

Replace =pathValidated= with a typed receipt bound to graph snapshot, ordered
edge identities, source/target, validation domain, and closure digest.

** Multi-objective policy

The current corrected router uses graph-first lexicographic order after budget
filtering. A later RFC may compare this with Pareto-frontier or
obligation-specific ordering, but it must not silently return to transition
count as a graph-distance proxy.
