* Problem

Evidence Graph, Graph Router, and Turbo Graph changes can be locally correct
while still being wrong for the shared search core.

A new graph edge may reduce hop count while increasing serialized evidence,
model input tokens, or agent interaction rounds.  A router state may improve
one company's workload while changing unrelated workloads.  A feature may be
easy to add but impossible to remove because its state, schema, cache identity,
and receipts become shared protocol commitments.

Implementation quality, passing CI, and local route improvement do not establish
shared value.

* Decision

An Evidence Graph or Graph Router extension MUST remain downstream or
experimental until it carries a =CoreAdmissionCertificate=.

The certificate proves all of:

1. independent shared value;
2. preserved reachability and evidence soundness;
3. bounded runtime and shared-complexity externality;
4. isolation from workloads that do not request the extension;
5. an explicit deletion retraction that restores baseline routing;
6. authorization of the policy that defines independence, affected scope, and
   budgets;
7. binding of evidence validity to an independently authorized verifier;
8. a bounded lifecycle with explicit review, expiry, and authorized renewal.

Core admission is therefore a proof obligation, not a reward for implementation
completeness.

* Evidence route

An evidence route contains:

- the evidence objects exposed to the agent;
- whether the route reaches the requested semantic goal;
- whether the exposed evidence is valid;
- graph-hop cost;
- serialized token cost;
- agent interaction-round cost.

The initial cost model is:

The componentwise admission relation is intentionally conservative.  A
trade-off that increases one component to improve another requires an explicit
policy profile and MUST NOT be silently described as a globally better route.

* Comparable measurement context

:PROPERTIES:
:RFC_CLAUSE_ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-EGA-MEASUREMENT
:END:

A baseline and extension measurement are comparable only when they bind the
same:

- committed evidence-graph snapshot;
- model profile;
- semantic-search cache state;
- model prefix-cache state;
- measurement protocol;
- uncertainty protocol.

Semantic-search caching and model prefix caching are distinct dimensions.
Receipts MUST NOT collapse them into an undifferentiated =cacheHit= field.

Cold and warm cohorts MAY both be measured, but each claimed improvement MUST
compare routes inside one cohort.  A warm extension compared with a cold
baseline is not certified evidence of router improvement.

Point estimates are insufficient when measurements vary.  A robust improvement
requires the candidate upper cost bound to be strictly better than the baseline
lower cost bound.  Overlapping intervals remain inconclusive rather than being
rounded into a positive result.

* Authorized workload coverage

:PROPERTIES:
:RFC_CLAUSE_ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-EGA-COVERAGE
:END:

The measurement scope MUST cover every workload in the committed admitted
workload universe.  The same external policy that authorizes budgets and
affected scope also authorizes the measured-workload predicate.

A proposer cannot establish shared value by measuring only workloads on which
the extension wins.  Additional sampling MAY be used for large universes, but
the sampling policy, uncertainty protocol, and coverage claim must be
contract-visible.

* Independent shared value

:PROPERTIES:
:RFC_CLAUSE_ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-EGA-VALUE
:END:

At least two admitted workloads whose owners satisfy an explicit independence
relation MUST receive a certified route improvement.

One internal workload, multiple benchmarks owned by the same organization, or
multiple prompts generated from one private use case do not establish
independent shared value.

A certified improvement requires baseline success, extended-route success,
valid extended evidence, and strict componentwise cost improvement.  A cheaper
failed route does not establish value.

This is a minimal admission rule, not proof that two users are sufficient for
permanent standardization.  Governance MAY require stronger adoption evidence.

* Admission-policy authority

:PROPERTIES:
:RFC_CLAUSE_ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-EGA-POLICY
:END:

The certificate MUST bind an independently authorized policy covering:

- the owner-independence relation;
- the affected-workload domain;
- the measured-workload domain;
- graph-hop, token, and interaction-round budgets;
- the shared-complexity budget.

The feature proposer MUST NOT satisfy this obligation merely by choosing a
larger budget, minting additional owner identifiers, or marking every workload
as affected.  Authorization is external evidence consumed by the proof.

The owner-independence relation MUST be irreflexive and symmetric.  The Lean
certificate therefore derives that the two beneficiary owners are distinct
instead of trusting identifier inequality as the definition of independence.

* Evidence-verifier binding

:PROPERTIES:
:RFC_CLAUSE_ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-EGA-EVIDENCE
:END:

The extension MUST bind its =evidenceValid= proposition to an independently
authorized verifier over:

- the exact workload and its semantic requirement;
- the complete measurement context, including graph snapshot identity;
- the exact evidence objects exposed by the route.

The binding is bidirectional.  A route cannot set =evidenceValid= to =True=
without satisfying the verifier, and the receipt cannot silently discard a
successful verifier result.

Verifier authorization is distinct from feature-policy authorization.  A
feature proposer MUST NOT define both the new evidence semantics and the only
verifier allowed to approve those semantics.

The verifier MUST distinguish:

- item soundness: every returned evidence object is valid;
- requirement completeness: every evidence obligation required by the workload
  is satisfied.

Returning one valid item while omitting another required item may reduce token
cost, but it is not a correct Evidence Graph route.

* Search correctness preservation

:PROPERTIES:
:RFC_CLAUSE_ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-EGA-CORRECTNESS
:END:

For every baseline workload that reaches its semantic goal, the extended router
MUST still reach that goal.

Every extended route that claims success MUST expose valid evidence.  A shorter
path to an unsupported conclusion is not an optimization.

* Bounded runtime externality

:PROPERTIES:
:RFC_CLAUSE_ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-EGA-RUNTIME
:END:

Every workload, including a workload that does not benefit, MUST remain within
explicit hop, token, and interaction-round budgets relative to baseline.

The budget is contract-visible.  It MUST NOT be hidden in router heuristics or
provider-private thresholds.

* Shared-complexity budget

:PROPERTIES:
:RFC_CLAUSE_ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-EGA-COMPLEXITY
:END:

The certificate records added:

- schema concepts;
- router states or transitions;
- long-lived maintenance obligations.

All components MUST remain within a declared shared-complexity budget.  Runtime
speed does not compensate automatically for permanent protocol complexity.

* Isolation

:PROPERTIES:
:RFC_CLAUSE_ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-EGA-ISOLATION
:END:

For workloads outside the declared affected domain, the extended route cost
MUST equal baseline.

The production contract SHOULD strengthen cost equality to packet, evidence,
cache-identity, and selected-route equality.  This RFC begins with the smallest
mathematical boundary needed to expose leakage.

* Deletion witness

:PROPERTIES:
:RFC_CLAUSE_ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-EGA-DELETION
:END:

Every admitted extension MUST define:

- the baseline and active extended states being certified;
- an embedding from baseline state into extended state;
- a removal projection from extended state to baseline state;
- an erasure operation on the actual extended state;
- a proof that removing an embedded baseline state returns that state;
- proofs binding the witness to the same baseline and extended routers used by
  the admission certificate;
- idempotent erasure;
- proof that the actual extended router restores baseline behavior after
  erasure.

The deletion state MUST cover the transitive causal closure of the feature,
including:

- router configuration and feature flags;
- semantic-search cache entries;
- learned route preferences or self-improvement state;
- derived graph indices and materialized shortcut edges.

“No current user complained” is not a deletion witness.

An unrelated retraction over unrelated states is also not a deletion witness.
Deleting code while leaving feature-derived state able to influence routing is
not deletion.

* Sunset and renewal

:PROPERTIES:
:RFC_CLAUSE_ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-EGA-SUNSET
:END:

Every core admission MUST include:

- admission time;
- mandatory review time;
- expiry time.

The ordering MUST satisfy:

#+begin_quote
admission time < review time <= expiry time
#+end_quote

Silence does not extend expiry.  After expiry the feature MUST enter deletion
or full re-admission unless a renewal receipt was issued within the previous
lifecycle.

=MustErase= is the logical negation of lifecycle validity.  For an expired
feature with no renewal receipt, the protocol MUST connect =MustErase= to
feature causal-closure erasure; setting a lifecycle label to =expired= without
removing route-influencing state is non-compliant.

A valid renewal receipt binds:

- current independent-value evidence;
- an active maintenance owner;
- the old and new expiry;
- external renewal authorization.

An arbitrary receipt, a receipt issued after expiry, or a longer date chosen by
the feature owner does not renew admission.

* Lean-discovered non-implications

:PROPERTIES:
:RFC_CLAUSE_ID: ASP-RFC-10.05-EGA-NONIMPLICATION
:END:

The Lean model MUST preserve counterexamples showing:

- fewer graph hops do not imply lower token or interaction cost;
- improvement for one owner does not establish independent shared value;
- bounded runtime regression does not imply isolation;
- an unchanged router is removable but has no admission value;
- lower measured cost under a different semantic-search or model-prefix cache
  context is not a certified improvement;
- a lower point estimate with overlapping uncertainty intervals is not a robust
  improvement;
- a favorable benchmark subset does not establish committed workload coverage;
- validity of every returned evidence item does not imply completeness of the
  workload's required evidence;
- deleting router code while retaining semantic cache, learned preference, or
  derived graph-index state does not restore baseline routing;
- silence and an unauthorized renewal receipt do not extend an expired feature.

These are protocol-design failures, not implementation bugs.

Lean model review also exposed two specification holes before compilation:

- cost improvement alone could count a failed or evidence-invalid route as
  value, so admission now requires =CertifiedImprovement=;
- a deletion witness could be constructed over routers unrelated to the
  admitted baseline and extension, so the witness now binds the active states,
  both router interpretations, and the actual erasure behavior;
- self-declared budgets and affected scopes could make externality and isolation
  obligations vacuous, so the certificate now requires an externally authorized
  admission policy;
- a route-local =evidenceValid= proposition could be set to =True= without
  validating the evidence, so the certificate now binds it to an independently
  authorized snapshot-aware verifier;
- code-only deletion could leave feature-derived caches, learned preferences,
  and graph indices active, so deletion now covers the feature's transitive
  causal state closure;
- a one-time admission could otherwise survive indefinitely through silence,
  so every certificate now carries a bounded lifecycle and renewal authority;
- point estimates and proposer-selected benchmark subsets could overstate
  improvement, so the policy now authorizes measurement coverage and the model
  requires non-overlapping robust cost bounds;
- valid returned items could hide omitted required evidence, so the verifier is
  now workload-aware and owns both evidence soundness and completeness.

* Graph Router consequence

A Graph Router MUST optimize a declared route-cost relation.  Hop count alone
is not an admissible global objective.

The router receipt MUST distinguish:

- selected route;
- alternatives considered or their committed frontier;
- evidence validity;
- hop, token, and interaction-round costs;
- policy profile when a non-componentwise trade-off is accepted;
- admission-certificate identity for any core extension used by the route.

* Python Turbo Graph boundary

The future Python Turbo Graph implementation owns efficient graph materialization,
ranking, and route execution.  It does not own the meaning of core admission.

Python MUST eventually emit sufficient inputs for independent verification:

- workload and owner identity class;
- committed evidence-graph snapshot identity;
- model-profile identity;
- semantic-search cache identity;
- model prefix-cache identity;
- measurement-protocol identity;
- uncertainty-protocol identity and cost intervals;
- admitted and measured workload-universe identities;
- baseline and extended route costs;
- evidence validity receipt;
- workload evidence-requirement identity and completeness result;
- evidence-verifier identity and authorization evidence;
- affected-domain decision;
- runtime and complexity budgets;
- deletion-witness version;
- feature-derived cache, learned-state, and graph-index deletion footprint;
- lifecycle policy and renewal-receipt identity;
- router policy identity.

Until that implementation exists, Lean proves the shared semantic boundary and
counterexamples only.

* Non-goals

- Implement Python Turbo Graph.
- Modify Rust search providers.
- Choose a permanent weighted route objective.
- Claim that two owners alone guarantee long-term value.
- Treat tests, CI, or implementation polish as core-admission evidence.
- Require every downstream experiment to satisfy core-admission obligations.
- Use raw LaTeX, standalone Typst, =:file=, or Typst compilation.

* Remaining proof boundary

The next layers should formalize:

- equality of unaffected evidence packets, not only route cost;
- evidence required to justify the declared owner-independence relation;
- multi-cycle renewal, maintenance-owner disappearance, and mandatory
  transition from expiry to causal-closure erasure;
- observational equivalence after deletion;
- a formal causal-closure witness proving that every persistent state capable
  of influencing routing is included in erasure;
- graph-frontier completeness under the declared route objective;
- distributional shift, sequential testing, and multiple-comparison correction
  beyond conservative cost intervals;
- cold/warm cohort aggregation without mixing incomparable cache contexts;
- authenticated binding between Python measurements and the committed evidence
  graph snapshot.
