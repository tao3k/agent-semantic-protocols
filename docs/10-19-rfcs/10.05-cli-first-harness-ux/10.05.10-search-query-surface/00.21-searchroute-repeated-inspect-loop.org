#+status: Draft

* Status

This RFC is a proof-driven extension of [[file:00.20-searchroute-partial-route-inspect-scheduler.org][RFC 10.05.20 — Partial-Route Inspect Scheduler]].
It specifies the repeated agent-facing inspect loop that consumes the
=inspect= frontier, materializes bounded evidence, may update the selected
candidate, and reschedules remaining work.

This session does not authorize Rust implementation. The normative artifacts
are this Org RFC, its embedded Typst model, the Lean model under
=packages/proofs=, and the Lean JSON plus AXLE audit receipts.

* Why a single scheduler pass is insufficient

RFC 10.05.20 proves that one scheduler pass safely partitions certified
frontiers into =pruned= and =inspect=. It intentionally leaves four dynamic
questions open:

1. how an inspected frontier is replaced by child frontiers;
2. how a newly discovered candidate may replace the current selection;
3. why repeated materialization and rescheduling cannot stutter forever; and
4. why loop termination does not silently discard candidates.

The Lean construction found that a fuel counter alone answers only the third
question. A loop may strictly consume fuel and still delete an unresolved
frontier. Such a loop terminates, but its selected candidate is not proven
globally optimal. Therefore every inspect step needs two independent
certificates:

- a *progress certificate* that strictly decreases remaining work; and
- a *refinement coverage certificate* that conserves the candidate universe.

These certificates must not be collapsed into one opaque implementation
status.

* State and agent-facing decision

** Work-bearing frontier
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       ASP-RFC-10.05-SIL-DECISION
:END:

A pending entry is a pair =(frontier, work-credit)=. The frontier is the
certified partial frontier of RFC 10.05.20. Its credit is a positive natural
number. A loop state contains exactly:

- the currently selected complete graph candidate; and
- an ordered list of work-bearing pending frontiers.

The agent-facing loop exposes only two typed decisions for the head frontier:

- =prune=, when the selected candidate is no worse than the head lower bound;
- =inspect(new-selected, children)=, when materialization returns a
  non-regressing selected candidate and zero or more certified child
  frontiers.

The protocol does not expose a growing grammar of search subcommands. A small
inspect response carries the next typed decision and only the evidence needed
for that decision. Router movement and model/tool turns remain explicit costs
of the routes represented by the candidates.

* Strict work decrease

** Progress certificate
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       ASP-RFC-10.05-SIL-WORK-DECREASE
:END:

Let =credit(F)= be the positive work credit of a pending frontier and let
=W(S)= be the sum of pending credits in state =S=.

A valid prune removes the head frontier. A valid inspect replaces the head
with =children= whose total credit is *strictly less* than the removed head
credit:

#+begin_example
sum(map credit children) < credit(head)
#+end_example

Consequently every valid decision satisfies =W(next(S)) < W(S)=. Non-strict
credit is forbidden. If equality were accepted, an inspect decision could
replace a one-credit frontier with itself, leave the complete state unchanged,
and create an infinite one-state loop.

Credit is a proof-relevant upper bound on remaining inspect work. It is not a
latency estimate, semantic-search cache hit, model prefix-cache hit, token
count, or graph distance. Those remain separate route-cost axes. A provider
may derive credit from a finite catalog or DAG rank, but the receipt must make
that derivation auditable.

* Candidate-universe conservation

** Refinement coverage
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       ASP-RFC-10.05-SIL-COVERAGE
:END:

For a fixed candidate-universe snapshot, the loop coverage invariant says that
every candidate is either:

1. already no better than the current selected candidate; or
2. contained in at least one pending certified frontier.

A prune preserves this invariant through the certified lower-bound pruning
theorem from RFC 10.05.19 and RFC 10.05.20.

An inspect must provide a =RefinementCovers= receipt. For every candidate in
the removed head frontier, the receipt proves that the candidate is either no
better than =new-selected= or appears in one of the child frontiers. Candidates
covered by the untouched tail remain pending. The new selected candidate must
also be no worse than the old selected candidate.

This is the conservation law that a fuel-only design was missing. An empty
child list is valid only when the new selected candidate dominates every
candidate covered by the removed head.

The candidate universe and its snapshot identity remain owned by the
=CandidateUniverse= and coverage receipts of RFC 10.05.19. Reusing a loop
receipt against a different source/index snapshot is invalid.

* Trace bound

** Finite repeated inspection
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       ASP-RFC-10.05-SIL-TRACE-BOUND
:END:

For a valid trace of =n= decisions from =S0= to =Sn=, Lean proves:

#+begin_example
W(Sn) + n <= W(S0)
n <= W(S0)
#+end_example

The bound counts loop decisions, not raw parser operations. A concrete cost
policy may additionally price each decision in model/tool rounds, router hops,
uncached tokens, semantic-search cache misses, and model prefix-cache misses.
Those costs must be accumulated without changing the termination measure.

This separation prevents a cache hit from being misreported as logical
progress. Search-result cache reuse can reduce evidence retrieval cost, while
model prefix-cache reuse can reduce prompt recomputation; neither by itself
removes a pending frontier.

* Typed terminal closure

** Closure and global optimality
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       ASP-RFC-10.05-SIL-TERMINAL
:END:

A well-formed state with zero remaining work has an empty pending list. A
closed state whose coverage invariant still holds has a selected candidate no
worse than every candidate in the bound universe.

For a valid trace whose final work is zero, Lean combines strict work
decrease, positivity, invariant preservation, and coverage conservation to
prove both:

- the final state is closed; and
- the final selected candidate is globally optimal for the bound candidate
  universe.

The theorem does not claim global optimality for candidates outside that
universe, for a stale snapshot, or for a provider that cannot emit the required
lower-bound and refinement receipts.

Using the complete initial work budget is a sufficient diagnostic corollary,
not the operational definition of completion. A single inspect may consume
more than one credit, so a successfully closed trace may be strictly shorter
than its initial work bound.

= State and measure

Let $C$ be a finite, snapshot-bound candidate universe. A loop state is
$S = (s, P)$, where $s in C$ is the selected complete candidate and $P$ is an
ordered list of certified pending frontiers. Each $F in P$ has a positive
credit $c(F) in NN_(> 0)$.

$ W(S) = sum_(F in P) c(F) $

The candidate order $prec.eq$ is lexicographic in validated graph hops,
uncached tokens, model/tool rounds, and loop transitions.

= Valid decisions

For $P = [F] + P_r$, a prune is valid when $s prec.eq L(F)$, where $L(F)$ is
the certified lower bound. Its successor is $(s, P_r)$.

An inspect returns $(s', K)$ and is valid only when

$ s' prec.eq s, quad sum_(G in K) c(G) < c(F). $

It must also carry refinement coverage:

$ forall x in F, quad s' prec.eq x quad or quad exists G in K: x in G. $

The successor is $(s', K + P_r)$.

= Progress theorem

For either valid decision,

$ W(S') < W(S). $

Therefore a valid trace $S_0 arrow.r.long S_n$ of $n$ decisions satisfies

$ W(S_n) + n <= W(S_0), quad n <= W(S_0). $

This is a bound on inspect-loop decisions. Graph hops, uncached tokens, rounds,
semantic-search cache misses, and model prefix-cache misses remain independent
cost coordinates.

= Coverage conservation

Define the loop invariant

$ Cover(C, S) := forall x in C:
  (s prec.eq x) or (exists F in P: x in F). $

Safe lower-bound pruning preserves $Cover$. Inspect preserves $Cover$ only
because its refinement receipt maps every candidate removed with $F$ either to
the improved selection $s'$ or to a child frontier in $K$.

= Terminal theorem

If credits are positive and $W(S_n) = 0$, then $P_n = emptyset$. If
$Cover(C, S_n)$ also holds, then

$ forall x in C, quad s_n prec.eq x. $

Thus closure plus conserved coverage proves global optimality over the bound
snapshot, not over an unstated open world.

= Counterexample to non-strict credit

If inspect were allowed to satisfy only

$ sum_(G in K) c(G) <= c(F), $

choose $K = [F]$, $c(F) = 1$, and $s' = s$. Then $S' = S$ and
$W(S') = W(S)$. The loop can repeat forever. Strict decrease is therefore a
protocol obligation, not an implementation optimization.

* Agent-facing implication

The default interaction is a progressive =inspect= loop, not a complex public
search command grammar:

#+begin_example
inspect state
  -> prune(head, lower-bound receipt)
  -> or inspect(head, bounded evidence, refinement receipt)
  -> update selected
  -> reschedule children
  -> typed closed result
#+end_example

The loop optimizes for the shortest validated route to sufficient evidence:
fewer graph hops, fewer uncached tokens, fewer model/tool rounds, and fewer
loop transitions. It does not hide these axes behind one cache or one scalar
score.
