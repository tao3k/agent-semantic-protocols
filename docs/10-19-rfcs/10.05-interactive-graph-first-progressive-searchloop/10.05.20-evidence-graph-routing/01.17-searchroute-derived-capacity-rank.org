#+title: SearchRoute Derived Capacity Rank
#+subtitle: Constructing the bounded rank from finite candidates and Pareto descent
#+startup: overview
#+options: toc:3

* Status

This module closes the certificate-construction gap left by
[[file:01.16-searchroute-capacity-rank-certificate.org][01.16]].

It is an RFC, Lean, and Typst proof stage only.  It does not authorize a Rust or
Python implementation.

* Result

The capacity-rank certificate no longer needs to be assumed.

For a finite ordered candidate universe =U=, define the normalized rank of a
candidate =r= as the number of universe entries whose scalar total cost is
strictly lower than the total cost of =r=.

#+name: derived-capacity-rank-model
#+begin_src typst :lint typst-eval-stdin :runtime typst
$ "totalCost"(r) =
  "graphHops"(r) +
  "projectedTokens"(r) +
  "interactionRounds"(r) +
  "searchCacheMisses"(r) +
  "modelCacheMisses"(r) $

$ rho_U(r) =
  |{x in U: "totalCost"(x) < "totalCost"(r)}| $

$ r in U
  quad "implies" quad
  rho_U(r) < |U| $
#+end_src

If =findDominator U removed = witness=, its existing soundness theorem provides
both:

- =witness= constructively appears in =U=;
- =witness= strictly Pareto-dominates =removed=.

Strict Pareto dominance makes every cost dimension no worse and at least one
dimension strictly better.  Therefore total cost strictly decreases.  Because
the witness itself appears in =U=, it contributes to the removed candidate's
lower-cost count but not to its own lower-cost count.

#+name: derived-capacity-rank-descent
#+begin_src typst :lint typst-eval-stdin :runtime typst
$ "findDominator"(U, r) = w
  quad "implies" quad
  "totalCost"(w) < "totalCost"(r) $

$ "findDominator"(U, r) = w
  quad "implies" quad
  rho_U(w) < rho_U(r) $

$ r in U
  quad "implies" quad
  "status"("resolve"(U, |U|, r)) = "retained" $
#+end_src

This constructs all fields required by the 01.16
=CapacityRankCertificate= and discharges the capacity-fuel theorem without a
trusted provider rank.

* Normative clauses

** DCR-SCALAR: executable natural comparisons are connected to propositions

The proof layer MUST connect the executable =natLe= and =natLt= functions to
the corresponding natural-number relations.

The current closure proves:

- =natLe left right = true= implies =left <= right=;
- =natLt left right = true= implies =left < right=;
- =left < right= implies =natLt left right = true=;
- executable =natLt= is transitive.

These bridges are constructive and do not require proposition extensionality.

** DCR-DESCENT: strict Pareto dominance lowers scalar total cost

For the canonical five-dimensional cost vector:

#+begin_example
graphHops
projectedTokens
interactionRounds
searchCacheMisses
modelCacheMisses
#+end_example

strict dominance MUST imply strict descent of the sum of all five dimensions.

This scalar is a proof measure.  It does not replace the Pareto policy used for
route selection and MUST NOT be used to rank incomparable candidates.

** DCR-MEMBER: selected witnesses carry constructive universe evidence

The existing =findDominator_some_is_sound= theorem returns an =Appears=
derivation for the selected witness.  The capacity proof MUST consume that
derivation rather than trust a detached witness identifier.

The proof also connects ordinary list membership to =Appears= so the initial
candidate can enter the same constructive boundary.

** DCR-RANK: normalized rank counts lower-cost universe entries

The canonical derived proof rank is:

#+begin_example
lowerCostCount candidates candidate
#+end_example

It counts entries, not distinct identifiers.  Its required properties are:

- it never exceeds the candidate list length;
- increasing the scalar threshold cannot decrease the count;
- a lower-cost candidate that appears in the universe has a strictly lower
  normalized rank.

The rank is intentionally proof-oriented.  A runtime MAY use a more efficient
equivalent certificate, but equivalence must be replayable against this
contract.

** DCR-BOUND: every member rank is below candidate capacity

If a candidate appears in =U=, that occurrence cannot satisfy
=totalCost candidate < totalCost candidate=.  Its normalized rank is therefore
strictly below =length U=.

Duplicate list entries do not invalidate this mathematical bound; they increase
the capacity and may weaken efficiency.  Identity uniqueness remains a
separate evidence-quality requirement.

** DCR-CERTIFICATE: construction replaces provider trust

The proof package MUST construct the 01.16 certificate from:

- the finite candidate list;
- =findDominator_some_is_sound=;
- strict-dominance total-cost descent;
- normalized lower-cost counting.

A provider-emitted rank without independent derivation is not equivalent to
this closure.

** DCR-RETAINED: finite-universe capacity fuel is unconditional

For a start candidate that belongs to the finite universe, resolution with
=length U= fuel returns =retained=.  No separately trusted rank premise remains
in the public theorem.

The theorem is unconditional relative to the executable candidate list and
resolver definitions.  It does not claim that an adapter supplied the intended
candidate universe; universe commitment remains an upstream evidence concern.

** DCR-ENDPOINT: the derived proof reaches a Pareto-kept endpoint

The derived certificate composes with the retained-endpoint theorem:

#+begin_example
keepCandidate U (resolve U (length U) start).endpoint = true
#+end_example

The SearchLoop may therefore return a compact endpoint receipt without exposing
each internal witness edge by default.

** DCR-CONSTRUCTIVE: audit closure excludes proposition extensionality

An initial proof used standard Boolean conjunction/disjunction equivalences and
thereby introduced =propext= into the axiom inventory.

The accepted proof uses explicit four-case Boolean eliminators.  The JSON audit
MUST report an empty axiom inventory for the derived certificate and both
capacity-fuel conclusions.

* SearchLoop consequences

** Internal graph hops are not model turns

The proof bounds internal dominance resolution by candidate capacity.  A public
adapter SHOULD perform this resolution inside one SearchLoop transition and
project:

#+begin_example
status
endpointId
steps
candidateUniverseDigest
derivedRankAlgorithmId
proofReceiptDigest
#+end_example

The model does not need one tool call per dominance witness.

** Explicit chains remain an audit projection

The endpoint receipt is sufficient for the normal hot path when its universe
and proof digests replay successfully.  Explicit witness chains remain useful
for:

- disputed routing decisions;
- verifier diagnostics;
- certificate-generation failures;
- offline graph-quality analysis.

** Cache dimensions remain separate

The derived rank may be stored in a search cache keyed by the committed
candidate universe and cost-vector semantics.  Stable endpoint receipts may
improve model prefix-cache reuse.  These are different events and MUST retain
separate measurements.

** Construction complexity is not hidden

A direct implementation of =lowerCostCount= for every candidate can be
quadratic in candidate count.  This proof establishes logical sufficiency, not
the preferred runtime algorithm.

Runtime work must compare:

- direct counting;
- one stable sort plus rank projection;
- incremental ranks attached to the evidence graph;
- cache reuse under candidate-universe digests.

Any optimization must preserve the derived certificate semantics.

* Logic findings

** Finding 1: topological sorting is not required for the capacity theorem

01.16 proposed a topological or longest-path rank as the likely construction.
Lean found a smaller proof boundary: finite membership plus strict scalar
descent is sufficient to construct a bounded normalized rank.

This removes a graph-wide topological-sort premise from the SearchLoop hot-path
contract.

** Finding 2: unique identifiers are not a termination premise

Unique canonical route identifiers are required for unambiguous evidence,
deduplication, digest replay, and useful capacity accounting.  They are not
consumed by the mathematical termination proof.

The RFC must not cite identity uniqueness as a theorem premise merely because
the runtime should enforce it for other reasons.

** Finding 3: full dominance transitivity is not required here

The derived rank proof uses transitivity of scalar natural-number order, not a
generic theorem that =StrictDominates= itself is transitive.

Full dominance transitivity is still required before a router may:

- replace a witness chain with a transitive dominance edge;
- omit intermediate dominance evidence;
- claim that an endpoint directly dominates every removed ancestor.

It belongs to the shortcut/compression proof boundary, not the capacity-fuel
boundary.

** Finding 4: the scalar rank does not authorize scalar route selection

Two incomparable Pareto candidates can have different total costs.  The scalar
measure is valid for proving descent along an already certified dominance edge,
but choosing the smaller total cost as the public route policy would silently
replace Pareto semantics.

* Next proof boundary

The next module should prove generic =StrictDominates= transitivity and connect
it to a safe transitive-shortcut projection:

1. prove componentwise =NoWorse= transitivity;
2. prove strict improvement survives composition with no-worse edges;
3. prove =StrictDominates a b= and =StrictDominates b c= imply
   =StrictDominates a c=;
4. prove that replacing an explicit witness chain by a direct endpoint edge
   preserves removal soundness;
5. distinguish constant-shape shortcut receipts from explicit chain audit
   receipts;
6. retain the Pareto policy even though scalar total cost remains available as
   a termination measure.

Until this closure exists, the SearchLoop may summarize a chain by endpoint and
proof digest, but MUST NOT claim a generic direct dominance edge that has not
been independently proved.
