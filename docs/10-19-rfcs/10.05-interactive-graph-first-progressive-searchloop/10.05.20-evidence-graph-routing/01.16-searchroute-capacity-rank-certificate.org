#+title: SearchRoute Capacity-Rank Certificate
#+subtitle: Axiom-free capacity fuel for internal dominance resolution
#+startup: overview
#+options: toc:3

* Status

This module is a proof-backed refinement of the bounded dominance witness
resolver introduced by [[file:01.15-searchroute-bounded-dominance-witness-chain.org][01.15]].

It changes no Rust or Python runtime.  It specifies the certificate required to
turn a generic termination argument into a candidate-capacity interaction
bound.

* Problem

01.15 proves that every selected witness edge has strict cost descent and that a
fuel-bounded resolver cannot take more steps than its supplied fuel.  Those
facts do not by themselves prove the stronger claim

#+begin_quote
candidate-count fuel is always sufficient to reach a retained endpoint.
#+end_quote

Strict descent in the raw =totalCost= rank proves well-foundedness, but the
initial total cost can be much larger than the number of candidates.  A
termination rank and a capacity-bounded rank are therefore different protocol
artifacts.

This distinction matters for an interaction-first SearchLoop:

- termination says that internal routing eventually stops;
- a capacity rank bounds how many graph-router transitions can occur;
- neither fact requires one model turn per transition;
- the public packet should summarize the internal chain unless an audit asks
  for explicit edges.

* Mathematical contract

Let =U= be the ordered candidate universe used by =findDominator=.  A
capacity-rank certificate provides a natural-valued rank for each candidate,
strict descent for every witness actually selected by the deterministic
resolver, and a rank bound below the candidate capacity.

#+name: capacity-rank-certificate-model
#+begin_src typst :lint typst-eval-stdin :runtime typst
$ "rank": U arrow.r "Nat" $

$ "findDominator"(U, r) = w
  quad "implies" quad
  "rank"(w) < "rank"(r) $

$ r in U
  quad "implies" quad
  "rank"(r) < |U| $
#+end_src

The executable resolver then admits the following bound.

#+name: capacity-rank-retained-endpoint
#+begin_src typst :lint typst-eval-stdin :runtime typst
$ r in U
  quad "implies" quad
  "status"("resolve"(U, |U|, r)) = "retained" $

$ "keep"(U, "endpoint"("resolve"(U, |U|, r))) = #true $

$ "steps"("resolve"(U, |U|, r)) <= |U| $
#+end_src

* Normative clauses

** CRC-RANK: the capacity certificate is explicit

A capacity-rank certificate MUST contain:

1. a rank function over candidates;
2. a proof that every witness selected by =findDominator= has a strictly lower
   rank than the removed candidate;
3. a proof that every candidate in the certified universe has rank strictly
   below the universe length.

The certificate is proof input.  A provider MUST NOT infer it from an
unrecorded heuristic.

** CRC-DESCENT: selected edges are the proof boundary

The kernel theorem requires descent only for the edge selected by the
deterministic resolver.  It does not require the public adapter to materialize
all pairwise dominance edges.

An implementation MAY retain a denser graph for diagnostics, but the compact
receipt SHOULD bind only:

- the candidate-universe digest;
- the deterministic selection policy;
- the capacity-rank certificate digest;
- the resolver result.

** CRC-BOUND: membership supplies the initial rank bound

For a start candidate in the certified universe, its rank is below
=candidateCount=.  The resolver therefore receives enough fuel when its fuel is
the universe length.

An empty universe cannot supply a valid start-member proof.  Empty-input
handling remains an adapter concern and MUST be represented as a typed outcome,
not as a fabricated retained candidate.

** CRC-RETAINED: capacity fuel cannot end as exhausted

Given a valid certificate and a member start candidate, resolution with
=candidateCount= fuel MUST return =retained=.

The =exhausted= status remains necessary for:

- deliberately smaller diagnostic fuel;
- an absent or rejected capacity certificate;
- replay against a candidate universe whose digest does not match the
  certificate.

** CRC-ENDPOINT: retained means Pareto-kept

The capacity theorem composes with the 01.15 endpoint theorem.  A retained
capacity-resolution endpoint MUST satisfy =keepCandidate U endpoint = true=.

This is stronger than returning a direct dominance witness.  A direct witness
may itself be removed, while the certified endpoint is retained.

** CRC-STEPS: graph transitions and model interactions are distinct

The resolver performs at most =candidateCount= internal witness transitions.
The public SearchLoop MUST NOT translate that bound into the same number of
LLM/tool round trips.

The normal projection SHOULD expose a constant-shape summary:

#+begin_example
status
endpointId
steps
candidateUniverseDigest
capacityRankCertificateDigest
#+end_example

Explicit witness edges SHOULD be an audit projection.  This preserves graph
reachability evidence while reducing projected tokens and model-loop turns.

** CRC-CACHE: cache claims bind different artifacts

A search cache may reuse the candidate universe, selected edges, or capacity
certificate.  A model prefix-cache hit may reuse the stable SearchLoop packet
prefix.  These are separate measurements.

The receipt MUST NOT report a generic =cacheHit=.  It SHOULD distinguish at
least:

- =searchCacheHit= for graph/search artifacts;
- =modelPrefixCacheHit= for model-serving prefix reuse.

A certificate digest improves both paths: it avoids retransmitting an explicit
chain and keeps the model-visible prefix stable.

** CRC-RECEIPT: default proof evidence is length-independent

The proof model distinguishes:

- an explicit receipt whose projected token cost grows with candidate count;
- a summarized certificate receipt with constant field count.

The summarized receipt proves the control decision by digest and theorem
identity.  It does not claim that certificate construction itself is constant
time.

* Logic findings

** Finding 1: termination rank is not an interaction bound

The 01.15 =totalCost= descent is a valid well-founded measure, but its numeric
value is not bounded by candidate count.  Conflating these two statements would
let the RFC claim a short SearchLoop path without proving it.

The capacity-rank certificate closes that gap explicitly.

** Finding 2: candidate identity uniqueness is not consumed by this kernel theorem

Once a valid bounded rank certificate is supplied, the retained-status theorem
uses:

- selected-edge descent;
- start membership;
- the member rank bound.

It does not consume unique candidate identifiers.  Identifier uniqueness is
still required for unambiguous evidence, digest construction, replay, and for a
future derivation of the certificate from the finite candidate graph.  It
SHOULD NOT be misreported as a premise of the smaller kernel theorem.

** Finding 3: certificate validation is still missing

This stage validates the consequences of a capacity certificate; it does not
yet construct that certificate from the evidence graph.  Trusting an
provider-emitted rank without replay would merely move the unverified claim.

The audit boundary therefore remains:

#+begin_example
candidate universe
-> selected witness relation
-> independently derived capacity rank
-> Lean theorem
-> compact resolver receipt
#+end_example

* Next proof boundary

01.17 should derive, rather than assume, the capacity-rank certificate.  The
next model should:

1. prove that =findDominator= returns a member of the candidate universe;
2. prove generic strict-dominance transitivity and irreflexivity;
3. construct a finite topological or longest-path rank for selected witness
   edges;
4. prove that the constructed rank is below candidate count;
5. bind candidate identity uniqueness to evidence and digest replay, while
   keeping it out of the smaller theorem when it is not logically consumed.

Until that derivation is axiom-free, the RFC may claim a conditional
capacity-fuel theorem, but MUST NOT claim that every runtime candidate universe
already carries a verified capacity certificate.
