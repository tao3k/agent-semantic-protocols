* Status and authority

This document composes three existing authorities rather than replacing them:

- [[file:00.35-feasibility-first-route-admission.org][RFC 00.35]] establishes that route budgets are hard feasibility boundaries and cost comparison occurs only after admission;
- [[file:01.25-searchroute-risk-bounded-batch-admission.org][RFC 01.25]] establishes expected batch cost, a separately certified tail envelope, a two-dimensional hard fallback cap, and risk-cache identity; and
- the canonical graph-router Pareto model keeps graph hops, interaction rounds, projected tokens, verification work, search executions, and model-prefix recomputations as distinct dimensions.

RFC 00.35 predates the risk envelope. Its route-local budget predicate cannot prove that a batch plan has a valid calibration, a tail certificate targeting the enforced hard cap, or worst-case round and provider-token safety. RFC 01.25 proves those facts for one batch plan, but does not yet state how the graph router must compose them with shortest-path and Pareto selection.

This RFC closes that seam. A candidate enters the optimization domain only after both route feasibility and risk-envelope feasibility are proved. Graph hops, expected savings, search-cache reuse, and model prefix-cache reuse are costs or observations; none is admission authority.

* Problem statement

The evidence-graph router is intended to minimize graph path length, agent/LLM interaction rounds, provider/search tokens, and repeated model work. That objective becomes unsafe when a candidate's batch plan is allowed to participate in ranking before its risk envelope is admitted.

Consider two routes:

| Route | Graph hops | Expected token cost | Hard fallback cap | Hard token-safe |
|-
| short | 1 | lower | 6 | no |
| longer | 2 | higher | 5 | yes |

For eight claims with two batch attempts, shared search input =S= ten, and per-claim input =P= two, cap six satisfies =g+h <= n= but violates the hard token condition: =(2+6)*10 + 6*2 = 92 > 80=. Cap five satisfies both the round and token hard bounds.

The one-hop route is lexicographically shorter. It may also have a model prefix-cache hit. Neither fact makes its fallback plan executable within the protocol budget. If hop-first or cache-adjusted ranking runs before risk admission, the router selects a route that must later overflow, fail closed, or degrade into unbounded fine-grained fallback commands.

The failure is not repaired by adding risk as another weighted score. Any finite weight permits a sufficiently large improvement in another dimension to compensate for a hard violation. Risk feasibility must restrict the domain before optimization.

* Lean-discovered logical gaps

** Route feasibility and risk feasibility are independent

RFC 00.35 route feasibility checks graph hops, uncached tokens, rounds, and transitions against route caps. A candidate can satisfy every one of those bounds while carrying a risk envelope whose hard fallback cap violates RFC 01.25 provider-token safety.

Conversely, a risk-safe batch plan can be attached to a graph route that exceeds its graph-hop or transition budget. Therefore neither predicate implies the other. Composite admission is their conjunction.

** Shortest graph path is conditional on admission

The phrase “shortest graph path” is underspecified unless it names the candidate universe and admission predicate. The router may claim shortest-path optimality only over the risk-feasible, route-feasible candidates covered by its catalog or frontier certificate.

An infeasible one-hop candidate is not a counterexample to an admitted two-hop optimum because it is outside the optimization domain. An omitted one-hop family is still a completeness obligation: the router must either enumerate it and reject it with a valid infeasibility certificate, or prove that every member of the omitted family violates a hard premise.

** Expected admission cannot reopen a hard boundary

RFC 01.25 =ExpectedCostAdmissible= supports plan generation and ranking. It is not a substitute for =ValidRiskEnvelope= or =HardFallbackSafe=. A route with attractive expected mass but an unsafe hard cap remains outside the graph optimization domain.

This separation also prevents an agent from repeatedly constructing smaller individual commands after a batch failure. Once the hard cap is reached, the route terminates with an overflow/fail-closed receipt. The graph router does not create a new implicit route generation merely because more fine-grained commands are available.

** Evaluation identity is a product, not a prefix

The existing route-evaluation identity binds workspace snapshot, query, search policy, model, prompt/tool surface, and route budget. RFC 01.25 risk-cache identity additionally binds plan, checkpoint, eligibility policy, calibration snapshot, search-cost profile, risk cells and their digest, hard cap, allowed tail policy, and tail certificate.

A comparable risk-aware evaluation requires both identities. Equality of the legacy route identity does not imply equality of the risk identity. In particular, the same graph route evaluated under a changed calibration or changed =S/P= search-cost profile is not reusable.

** Cache improvements are monotone costs, not admission evidence

A model prefix-cache hit can lower =modelPrefixRecomputations= or effective model input. A semantic search-cache hit can lower =searchExecutions= and may eliminate provider work. These changes can improve a cost vector after their own identities are validated.

They do not prove that the risk envelope is current. A search-cache substitution that changes provider execution, batch composition, checkpoint, or risk cells requires a matching risk identity or a newly admitted envelope. A model prefix-cache hit never changes provider fallback probability or hard provider-token cost.

** Frontier closure must quantify over the admitted residual

A frontier lower-bound certificate ranks costs; it does not prove risk feasibility. Safe closure has two cases:

1. a risk or route infeasibility certificate excludes every candidate in the frontier; or
2. a lower-bound certificate proves the selected admitted route no worse than every admitted member of the frontier.

Applying a cost lower bound to a mixture of admitted and unclassified candidates is insufficient. The unclassified candidates still require risk-envelope resolution or a fail-closed exclusion proof.

* Mathematical model

Let =C= be a finite graph-route candidate catalog. Each candidate =c= carries graph cost =K(c)=, route-budget evidence, and a risk envelope =E(c)=. Define:

- =F_route(c)=: the canonical route budget and capability predicate;
- =F_risk(c)=: valid RFC 01.25 envelope, tail-to-cap binding, expected gates, and two-dimensional hard cap;
- =A(c)=: composite admission; and
- =C_A=: the admitted subcatalog.

The following inline Typst block is the normative mathematical presentation. It is extracted as Org-Babel content and checked through =typst eval --in -=. No generated =.typ= file is an authority.

#+begin_src typst

= Risk-feasible graph selection

Composite admission is a conjunction:

$ A(c) = F_("route")(c) and F_("risk")(c). $

The optimization domain is filtered before comparison:

$ C_A = { c in C | A(c) }. $

For cost vector

$ K(c) = (H(c), R(c), T(c), V(c), S(c), M(c)), $

the selected route satisfies

$ c^* in C_A, $

$ forall c in C_A, quad K(c^*) <=_("Pareto") K(c). $

A shorter path does not compensate for failed admission:

$ H(c_s) < H(c_f), quad not A(c_s), quad A(c_f) $

$ ==> quad "selectable"(c_f) and not "selectable"(c_s). $

Risk-aware comparison binds both identity components:

$ I_("composite")(c) = (I_("route")(c), I_("risk")(c)). $

$ I_("route")(a) = I_("route")(b) $

$ not ==> I_("composite")(a) = I_("composite")(b). $

Cache credits change cost only after admission:

$ "cache-credit"(c) ==> K'(c) <=_("componentwise") K(c), $

$ "cache-credit"(c) not ==> A(c). $
#+end_src

The Pareto statement intentionally applies only to =C_A=. If =C_A= is empty, selection returns no executable route and an explicit admission failure. It does not fall back to the least-infeasible candidate.

* Normative protocol

** ASP-RFC-10.05-RFSG-FEASIBILITY-CONJUNCTION

=RiskRouteAdmitted(candidate)= MUST require both canonical route feasibility and a valid RFC 01.25 risk envelope. Neither predicate may be inferred from the other.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-RFSG-RISK-BEFORE-COST

The graph router MUST resolve risk admission before hop-first, lexicographic, Pareto, or weighted comparison. Infeasible or unresolved candidates are outside the optimization domain.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-RFSG-NO-HOP-COMPENSATION

Fewer graph hops MUST NOT compensate for a failed calibration identity, tail certificate, hard round cap, hard provider-token cap, route budget, or capability premise.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-RFSG-EXPECTED-RANKING-ONLY

Expected fallback mass and expected cost MAY rank alternative plans whose complete hard admission already holds. Expected evidence MUST NOT authorize a candidate whose hard or tail evidence is missing or invalid.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-RFSG-IDENTITY-PRODUCT

Risk-aware route comparison and cache reuse MUST bind both the route-evaluation identity and RFC 01.25 risk-cache identity. Equality of one component alone is insufficient.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-RFSG-CACHE-NONAUTHORITY

Search-cache and model prefix-cache credits MAY improve their distinct cost coordinates only after their identities are verified. A cache hit MUST NOT synthesize risk admission, repair a stale calibration, or change the hard fallback cap without a new envelope.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-RFSG-CONDITIONAL-SHORTEST

Any shortest-path or Pareto-optimality claim MUST name the admitted candidate universe and its coverage certificate. The claim is conditional on route realization, risk admission, and catalog/frontier completeness.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-RFSG-FRONTIER-CLOSURE

An omitted frontier may close only through a universal infeasibility certificate or a cost lower bound quantified over its admitted members. Minimum graph-hop count alone cannot close an unresolved risk frontier.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-RFSG-NO-LEAST-INFEASIBLE

When no candidate is admitted, the router MUST emit an explicit no-route/admission receipt. It MUST NOT select the least-infeasible route, silently widen the hard cap, assume independence, relabel a failure as agent preference, or start hidden individual fallback loops.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-RFSG-GENERATION-FENCE

Changing graph generation, route budget, plan digest, calibration snapshot, search-cost profile, risk cells, hard cap, or tail certificate MUST invalidate the composite evaluation. A new generation requires a new admission decision before ranking.

* Candidate and selection contract

A =RiskRouteCandidate= carries:

| Field family | Required meaning |
|-
| route identity | workspace, query, policy, model, prompt/tool, route budget |
| graph cost | hops, interactions, projected tokens, verification, search, model recomputation |
| route evidence | budget and capability feasibility |
| risk identity | plan, checkpoint, policy, calibration, cost profile, cells digest, cap, tail policy, tail certificate |
| risk evidence | valid envelope plus exact hard round/token bounds |
| catalog identity | candidate generation and coverage domain |

=RiskRouteAdmitted= is a proposition, not a numeric score. =RiskAdmittedNoWorse(left,right)= requires =left= admitted and either =right= not admitted or =left.cost= Pareto-no-worse than =right.cost=.

A selection receipt contains:

- selected candidate membership in the named catalog;
- route-feasibility evidence;
- risk-envelope evidence;
- composite evaluation identity;
- comparison evidence against every admitted visible candidate;
- frontier coverage and closure evidence; and
- an explicit empty-admitted-set outcome when no candidate is selectable.

* Cache boundaries

Three cache-related facts remain separate:

- semantic search-result reuse can change the executable graph route and provider work;
- search-risk reuse validates an RFC 01.25 envelope under an exact risk identity; and
- model prefix reuse changes model-side recomputation or uncached prompt cost.

A semantic result hit does not automatically preserve risk identity because it may have been produced under a different checkpoint, provider execution plan, or calibration snapshot. A risk-cache hit does not prove a model prefix hit. A model prefix hit does not reduce provider fallback mass, provider hard-token requirements, or tail probability.

* Agent-facing SearchLoop behavior

The graph router exposes a compact progressive decision rather than a complex public command tree:

1. enumerate or refine a bounded candidate/frontier set;
2. resolve route feasibility;
3. resolve or reject the risk envelope;
4. remove candidates that fail either hard predicate;
5. compare the admitted residual using the canonical cost vector;
6. return the selected route, its composite identity, and the next bounded action;
7. enforce the RFC 01.25 fallback cap during execution; and
8. reconcile realized outcomes through RFC 01.24 without reopening selection inside hidden loops.

This ordering reduces wasted LLM rounds: the model does not inspect or execute a nominally short candidate that the router already knows cannot remain within hard cost bounds.

* Lean proof obligations

The project Lean module proves:

- composite admission projects both route and risk feasibility;
- a one-hop route can be route-feasible and hop-first preferred while risk-infeasible;
- a two-hop risk-feasible route is admitted over that shorter route;
- expected or cache-improved cost does not synthesize risk feasibility;
- fallback cap six fails the provider-token boundary while cap five passes;
- equality of legacy route identity does not prove composite identity;
- calibration and search-cost changes invalidate composite comparison;
- every selected candidate is risk feasible; and
- an empty admitted catalog produces no selected candidate.

The audit remains modular and emits theorem families, clause bindings, and axiom inventory as JSON.

* AXLE independent challenge

AXLE checks a standalone Mathlib model under =lean-4.32.0=. Its separately verified theorem states that hop-first preference does not imply risk admission. The model also checks the cap-six token counterexample, cap-five closure, feasibility-first selection, cache nonauthority, and identity separation.

* Consequences for later shared schema and Python graph work

The later shared schema must represent route feasibility, risk feasibility, composite identity, and selection evidence as separate typed fields. A single =score=, =safe=true= Boolean, combined cache counter, or provider-private fallback heuristic cannot represent this RFC.

Future Turbo Graph Python work must consume the shared candidate and envelope contract. It must not rank NetworkX/SciPy/GraphBLAS paths first and attach safety afterward. Language providers may choose different graph engines, but they must reproduce the same admission-before-cost semantics and receipts.

* Non-goals

- This RFC does not implement Rust or product Python code.
- It does not add a public CLI command family.
- It does not place calibration estimation inside Lean.
- It does not turn risk into a weighted cost dimension.
- It does not claim global shortest-path completeness without a catalog/frontier coverage proof.
- It does not merge semantic search caching with model prefix caching.

* Open implementation evidence

A real-project evaluation must record total candidates, route-infeasible count, risk-infeasible count, unresolved-risk count, admitted count, frontier closure reasons, selected graph hops, provider/search tokens, agent/LLM rounds, search-cache observations, model prefix-cache observations, hard-cap overflow events, and whether every omitted family has a typed closure certificate.
