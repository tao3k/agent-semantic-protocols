#+title: 00.62 Completion-Equivalent Pareto Router
#+startup: overview

#+property: rfc_layer evidence-graph-routing

* Status

Draft.

This module connects SearchLoop potential, certified completion, and the
multi-dimensional graph cost model.  It specifies when the Graph Router may
safely prune one route as dominated by another.

* Discovered Design Gap

Three attractive local rules are insufficient:

1. choose the route with fewer graph hops;
2. choose the first action with the largest potential decrease per token;
3. choose the route with the largest cache hit.

A locally efficient first action can leave an expensive completion tail.  A
one-hop route can return a much larger projection than a three-hop route.  A
cached route can reuse a stale or incomplete completion certificate.

Cost dominance is sound only when both routes realize the same completion
obligation.

* Canonical Route Plan

A route plan contains:

- initial potential;
- final potential;
- completion key;
- graph hops;
- tool interaction rounds;
- search payload tokens;
- uncached model-work tokens.

The completion key commits to:

- generation;
- coverage digest;
- completion mode and gap.

Two routes are completion-equivalent only when their initial potential, final
potential, and completion key are equal.

* Cost Dominance

=CostNoWorse(a, b)= holds when every cost dimension of =a= is at most the
corresponding dimension of =b=.

=Dominates(a, b)= holds only when:

- =a= and =b= are completion-equivalent;
- =CostNoWorse(a, b)=;
- at least one cost dimension of =a= is strictly smaller.

Dominance is irreflexive and transitive.  A dominated route can be removed from
the admissible frontier without changing the completion result.

Routes with different completion keys or final potential are incomparable,
even if one is cheaper in every cost dimension.

* Why Local Efficiency Is Not a Proof

In the mathematical example:

- the greedy first action reduces potential by 2 for 1 token;
- the alternative first action reduces potential by 1 for 2 tokens;
- the greedy full route costs 101 search tokens;
- the alternative full route costs 4 search tokens.

The first action has the better local ratio, but the complete route is much
more expensive.  Therefore local potential decrease per token is a search
ordering heuristic, not a dominance certificate.

* Pareto Frontier

The Router maintains the non-dominated frontier of admissible,
completion-equivalent plans.

If several plans remain incomparable, policy may choose among them using an
explicit preference such as:

- token-first;
- round-first;
- latency-first;
- monetary-budget-first.

The preference MUST be declared in the route request or policy digest.  Hidden
scalar weights cannot justify cross-provider pruning because another provider
may assign different units or cache semantics.

* Budget Feasibility

Budget admission precedes preference:

- graph-hop cap;
- tool-round cap;
- search-token cap;
- uncached model-work cap.

A two-hop plan can fail token and model-work budgets while a three-hop plan
passes.  Hop count alone is not feasibility.

* Cache Semantics

Search-result caching and model-prefix caching affect different dimensions.

- A search-result cache may reduce graph work, tool rounds, or search payload.
- A model-prefix cache reduces uncached model work.
- Neither cache changes the completion key or final potential.
- Cache credits MUST be bounded by the corresponding planned dimension.
- A cache hit cannot make an incomplete route completion-equivalent to an exact
  route.

Cache-parametric costs can be compared only after both routes use the same
accounting contract and completion identity.

* Python Turbo Graph Consequences

A future Python router should:

- group candidates by completion key and potential endpoints;
- apply feasibility gates;
- compute a Pareto frontier within each group;
- retain incomparable plans;
- apply a declared selection policy;
- emit a pruning receipt with exact dominance witnesses.

The implementation should not rank all routes with one hidden floating-point
score.

* Lean Obligations

The accompanying Lean model establishes:

1. =ASP-RFC-10.05-CEP-COST=: cost-no-worse is reflexive and transitive;
2. =ASP-RFC-10.05-CEP-EQUIVALENCE=: completion equivalence is transitive;
3. =ASP-RFC-10.05-CEP-DOMINANCE=: dominance is irreflexive and transitive;
4. =ASP-RFC-10.05-CEP-COMPLETION=: dominance preserves completion identity;
5. =ASP-RFC-10.05-CEP-HOPS=: fewer graph hops do not imply full cost dominance;
6. =ASP-RFC-10.05-CEP-GREEDY=: better local efficiency does not imply a better
   complete route;
7. =ASP-RFC-10.05-CEP-INCOMPLETE=: a cheaper incomplete route cannot dominate
   an exact route;
8. =ASP-RFC-10.05-CEP-BUDGET=: a longer graph path can satisfy the complete
   budget while the shorter path fails;
9. =ASP-RFC-10.05-CEP-PRUNE=: a dominated route is not Pareto minimal.

The Lean kernel audit reports five axiom-free declarations and six
axiom-dependent declarations.  Cost transitivity, completion-equivalence
transitivity, dominance transitivity, completion preservation, and Pareto
pruning are axiom-free.  The complete inventory is =propext= and =Quot.sound=;
no declaration depends on =sorryAx=.

The model does not yet prove:

- completeness of a concrete route enumerator;
- optimality under a declared lexicographic policy;
- cache-credit derivation from runtime receipts;
- expected latency under stochastic provider behavior.
