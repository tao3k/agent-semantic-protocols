* Status and authority

This RFC is canonical for vector-coverage reuse across a graph-generation edge
that changes the transition authority.

It refines
[[file:01.13-searchroute-coverage-transition-chain-integrity.org][coverage transition-chain integrity]].
The stable-authority chain remains the simpler admission path. This RFC applies
only when a registry successor changes =authorityDigest=.

* Lean-discovered authority gaps

Two independent omissions make a generation-only transition unsafe during
authority rotation.

First, =CoverageHeader= binds =issuedGeneration= but not the complete registry
clock domain. Two domains can have the same epoch and different authority
digests. Therefore generation equality cannot identify which authority and
registry snapshot issued the coverage proof.

Second, the registry transition certificate is signed by the old authority.
That signature proves authorization to leave the old domain; it does not prove
that the new authority accepted responsibility for the successor domain.

A predicate that checks only the old signature admits a transfer whose
=newAuthorityAccepted= field is false. Lean constructs this counterexample and
proves that the full rotation predicate rejects it.

* Three-role authority model

An authority-changing edge has three separately bound roles:

1. the registry transition certificate is signed by the old authority;
2. the authority-transfer certificate is approved by the old authority and
   accepted by the new authority;
3. the coverage transition is served under the new authority.

These roles MUST NOT be collapsed into one ambiguous =authorityDigest=.

The existing coverage field is interpreted as the authority of the destination
domain. The registry certificate retains its existing interpretation as a
signature by the source-domain authority.

* Origin-domain binding

Rotation-safe reuse wraps the coverage header in an
=AuthorityBoundCoverageHeader= containing the complete issued
=ClockDomain=:

- registry snapshot digest;
- authority digest;
- epoch.

The domain epoch MUST equal =CoverageHeader.issuedGeneration=. Equality of the
epoch alone is insufficient.

* Normative clauses

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ARCC-ORIGIN-DOMAIN

The issuer of a rotation-reused coverage proof MUST be identified by the full
registry clock domain. A bare issued generation MUST NOT select an origin
authority.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ARCC-DUAL-AUTHORIZATION

Every authority-changing edge MUST carry an authority-transfer certificate
whose source and destination domains equal the registry transition domains.
Both =oldAuthorityApproved= and =newAuthorityAccepted= MUST be true.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ARCC-SIGNATURE-ROLES

The registry certificate authority MUST equal the source-domain authority. The
coverage transition authority MUST equal the destination-domain authority.
These equalities have different meanings and MUST be checked independently.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ARCC-ONE-SIDED-REJECTION

Old-authority approval without new-authority acceptance, or new-authority
acceptance without old-authority approval, MUST reject rotation reuse.

An old-authority registry signature does not imply successor acceptance.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ARCC-SEMANTIC-CONTINUITY

The transfer certificate, source registry domain, destination registry domain,
coverage header, and current coverage context MUST bind the same semantic
coverage identity.

Authority rotation MUST NOT be used to bridge a semantic identity change.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ARCC-CHAIN-CONTINUITY

An authority-rotation chain MUST project to the certified registry transition
chain. It retains exact successor continuity:

#+begin_example
startEpoch + edgeCount == endEpoch
#+end_example

Connected rotation-chain fragments may compose only at an identical registry
clock domain.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ARCC-FORK-PREVENTION

The registry successor relation MUST remain functional during rotation. Two
valid authority-rotation edges with the same source domain MUST have the same
destination domain.

Dual signatures do not repair a forked successor history.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ARCC-ENDPOINT-ADMISSION

The final registry-domain epoch and authority MUST equal the current
=graphGeneration= and =transitionAuthorityDigest=. The origin domain MUST equal
the domain embedded in the authority-bound coverage header.

** ASP-RFC-10.05-ARCC-GLOBAL-LIFT

Only authority-rotation-chain-admitted vector coverage may lift frontier-local
Pareto minimality to global Pareto minimality across an authority change.

* Failure behavior

The complete chain fails closed when:

- the issued header has no full origin domain;
- a transfer certificate omits either domain;
- registry, transfer, and coverage endpoints disagree;
- either authority has not consented;
- the coverage edge names the old authority as its serving authority;
- semantic identity changes during transfer;
- the registry successor relation forks;
- the final authority or epoch differs from the current context.

Failure invalidates reuse of the old coverage proof. It does not delete source
evidence and does not prevent rebuilding coverage under the accepted successor
authority.

* Remaining boundary

Dual Boolean authorization establishes the logical role split, but an
executable protocol must bind each consent to a signature scheme, signer key
epoch, canonical transfer digest, and replay domain. Those cryptographic
bindings require a separate proof before a transfer receipt can be accepted
across key rotation or signature-algorithm migration.
